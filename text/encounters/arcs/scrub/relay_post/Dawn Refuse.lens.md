# Lens — Dawn, Walking Away

How the PC sees this scene. Steering for color: whose eye, what to notice,
in what key. Stance, not facts.

## Perceptual
First light through the cleared dust, and the PC already half on the road. The
eye is a traveller's, disengaging: picking up the pack without speaking, Devra
at her desk and Ossal at the brazier, neither of them looking up, neither
trying to keep the PC. Outside, the quiet of the mesa after the storm. Notice
the practical things a traveller notices when leaving — the clean air, the
direction of the road, whether there is food enough to reach the next
settlement — and set against them the one larger thing that will not settle: a
message predicted the PC's arrival here, timestamped before they decided to
shelter, sent from no origin anyone can name. What happens between Devra and
Ossal after the door closes is no longer the PC's business, and the eye
declines to follow it.

## Social / tonal
Detachment, and the cold comfort of staying outside a thing. The register is a
traveller leaving a matter unresolved — not callous, simply not theirs to
resolve. The one note that follows the PC out the door is the predicted-
arrival knowledge, carried like grit under the collar: faint, unexplained,
not dramatized. Hold the paradox plainly rather than building it into horror —
the system knew they were coming; the system knows nothing; both are true.
Keep the supernatural to that single unresolved fact and let the rest be road,
weather, and the small relief of a traveller who has decided this was not
their fight.

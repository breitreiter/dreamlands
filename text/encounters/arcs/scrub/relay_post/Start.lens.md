# Lens — The Dust Storm (Arrival)

How the PC sees this scene. Steering for color: whose eye, what to notice,
in what key. Stance, not facts.

## Perceptual
A guild merchant caught alone on the open mesa road as a dust storm comes up
out of the south at the end of a day's travel. The first half is read with a
traveller's body: the light going wrong, then the air, grit working under the
collar, the rust-colored sky, the flat scrub with nowhere to shelter, and the
plain physical relief of a stone building resolving out of the murk. The
second half is a stranger's eye inside a Kesharat relay post — one stone room,
a desk, a brass receiver in its cradle, neat stacks of filed transcriptions, a
brazier with a teapot, a signaling rig on the roof made useless by the storm.
Name the machinery in the traveller's own renaissance-era words; the PC has no
proper term for any of it. Read Devra the way a trader reads a host: ink on
her fingers, four years of competence, the slightly manic warmth of someone
starved for company who talks too much. When she shows the wire messages,
notice that the sheets look procedurally perfect and describe impossible
things. At the end, an old man comes in out of the storm and the room
stiffens; read that change without yet knowing its cause.

## Social / tonal
An opening, and mostly warm. Color's job here is baseline normal — shelter,
tea, company after a hard road — tipping quietly off-true. The hospitality is
real and unforced: Devra is genuinely glad of the PC, and a killing storm
makes the warmth honest. The off-key note is faint and lives in the
*paperwork*, not the people: the content of the orders, the second private
notebook she should not be keeping, the too-much of her talk. Keep the cosmic
note nearly absent; nothing threatens, nothing moves wrong. Record the seams
(orders that read like ordinary memos and ask for the impossible, an operator
quietly afraid of her own files) and let the evening to come do the rest.

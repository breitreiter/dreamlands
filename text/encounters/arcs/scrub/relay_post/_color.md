# Color bible — The Relay Post

This file holds **only recurring motifs**: the few textures the arc returns
to across its scenes. They are available to any scene's grab bag where they
fit, rendered fresh and never forced; they are the threads that tie the six
encounters into one arc.

**What does NOT belong here:** per-scene and per-paragraph color, i.e. any
detail that appears in only one place (the bones of the old fence, Devra's
ink-stained fingers, Ossal's heavy oilskin, the cot in the storeroom, the
specific absurd orders read out one by one, the torn pennant). colorize
generates that per scene from the lens and the beats. The test for inclusion:
*do we come back to it?* If not, it is not a motif.

The per-scene lens sets each scene's stance; these motifs are the shared
substance underneath it.

Format: each `- ` entry is a motif. The bold phrase + sentence is the
canonical invariant (keep it fixed); the `render:` line is how to instance it
fresh. A `only-in:` line scopes a callback to named scenes; absent means
available everywhere.

Register note: Tier 2 scrub, a Kesharat relay post. The horror here is
**administrative and ambient**, never gore: orders that look exactly like
ordinary paperwork. The one openly supernatural beat (the wire coming alive)
and the one supernatural fact (the message that predicted the arrival) are
faint and isolated. Everything else is two honest people, a stone room, and a
storm. Keep the cosmic note small; let the paperwork carry the dread.

## Recurring motifs

- **the wire in its cradle** — the relay receiver: a small brass instrument
  resting in a cradle on Devra's desk, with a signaling apparatus on the roof
  that the storm has made useless. The PC has no proper word for the machine
  and names it in renaissance-era terms. Across the arc it mostly *ticks once
  and goes quiet* — its silence is the rule, which is why the one time it does
  not stay quiet matters. It is the arc's spine object: every message comes
  down it.
  render: the apparatus as a plain object in this beat (the cradle, the single
  dry tick, the quiet after, the dead rooftop rig in the storm). Name it in
  the PC's own words; never reach for telecom jargon.

- **the paperwork that looks correct** — carbon transcription sheets carrying
  the standard wire header from the regional office, kept in neat filed
  stacks: procedurally perfect documents that describe impossible things. The
  arc's central horror is bureaucratic — an order looks exactly like every
  other order and asks for a resonance platform at empty coordinates, four
  hundred units of a material no catalog lists, a transfer for a person whose
  number fits no format. The motif recurs as the official file, as Devra's
  private annotation log, and as the wax-paper sleeve of duplicate copies.
  render: a document in this beat — the carbon sheet, the correct header sat
  over wrong content, the flat administrative phrasing of the impossible, the
  log's cramped annotations, the sleeve carried or hidden or burned.

- **the storm as the clock** — the dust storm frames the whole arc and marks
  its hours: rust-colored sky and stinging grit at arrival, a muffled howl
  against the outer wall through the night, a drop to a low wind in the small
  hours, then cleared to gray-pink first light by dawn. Each scene reads the
  weather to know what time it is.
  render: the storm's state in this beat (driving grit and rust light; the
  howl muffled through stone; the drop to a low wind; the clean cleared air at
  dawn). Use it to mark the hour, not for spectacle.

- **the brazier and the tea** — Devra's brazier and teapot, the post's one
  source of warmth and the engine of its hospitality. Tea is poured for the
  guest on arrival; Ossal takes his cup there; through the long night the men
  stand at its coals. The same coals are where duplicate transcriptions burn
  in one ending. Domestic warmth that can turn.
  render: the brazier or a cup in this beat — tea poured for a guest, a cup
  set down, a man standing at the coals with his hands behind his back, the
  smell of burning paper if it comes to that. Keep it small and domestic.

- **the two certainties** — Devra and Ossal hold opposed certainties in one
  stone room: she has evidence and no framework, he has a framework and meets
  the evidence selectively. Their disagreement is worn smooth by long
  repetition and surfaces in small cross-room reactions, not open argument —
  her audible exhale when he reaches a familiar phrase, his patience like a
  priest hearing a doubting parishioner. Never an "argument aura"; staged in
  observed beats.
  render: a cross-room reaction in this beat — one of them reacting to the
  other from across the room while the PC deals with the first, the worn
  quality of a fight had many times before.
  only-in: The Relay Post, Midnight

## Callbacks

- **the message that predicted the arrival** — a routine maintenance notice
  carrying the post's own coordinates, timestamped before the PC arrived and
  bearing no origin header: it did not come from the capital, it came down the
  wire from the system itself, and it names the PC as "expect transient
  personnel." The cold seam the whole evening turns on, and the one fact that
  follows the PC out of the arc on the route where they walk away.
  render: the notice as a sheet (routine maintenance phrasing, the coordinates
  that are this building, the timestamp from before arrival, the missing
  origin header), or as the thing the PC cannot stop turning over on the road
  out. The system knew they were coming; the system knows nothing; both are
  true.
  only-in: The Relay Post, Dawn Refuse

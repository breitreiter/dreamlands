# Lens — Dawn, Devra Leaves

How the PC sees this scene. Steering for color: whose eye, what to notice,
in what key. Stance, not facts.

## Perceptual
The storm has cleared in the night; first light is gray-pink across the mesa
and the air is clean for the first time in a day. The eye watches a departure
and then a rupture. Read Devra at the door — pack on her shoulder, the sealed
guild letter in her hand, the wax-paper sleeve of duplicates sliding inside
her coat — and the flat, undramatic fact of her walking west toward Aldgate
without a goodbye and without looking back at the wire. Then the eye turns to
Ossal: an old man watching her go, holding it, then losing it — cursing her by
name and family and office, swearing patrols and chains, and turning the
curses on the PC with insinuation and a warning about the Trade Guild. Hold
through that to the last beat, where the brass receiver ticks once in its
cradle, then again, then comes alive in a long sequence neither of them
understands, and the old man falls silent. Notice the pennant torn half off
its pole by the storm.

## Social / tonal
Aftermath and rupture, ending on the single openly supernatural beat of the
arc. The scene opens quiet — a woman leaving at first light, a small human
kindness already done — and turns to an old man's rage before it lands on the
wire. Let the wire-coming-alive be the cold note: render it faint but
unmistakable, the system speaking at length with no human hand at the key, and
let it silence Ossal rather than threaten anyone. The PC's choice here was
modest — a letter, five minutes of guild form — and its cost is concrete: the
relay goes dark behind them. Keep the supernatural restraint; the dread is in
the machine talking to no one, not in spectacle.

# Lens — Dawn, Securing the Post

How the PC sees this scene. Steering for color: whose eye, what to notice,
in what key. Stance, not facts.

## Perceptual
Dawn, the storm down to nothing, the post being secured against the woman who
runs it. The eye is cold and practical, reading a room held under quiet
duress: Ossal setting his cup down and waiting on the PC's lead, the wire
ticking once, Devra very still at her desk and watching both men. Across the
branches the eye stays on concrete action — crossing the room to tell her
plainly she is to stand down; or working a scheme, drawing her attention to
one side of the desk while Ossal lifts the wax-paper sleeve from her pack on
the other; or, with no copies to find, turning the whole post over for an hour
and finding nothing. Read Devra throughout as a competent woman waiting for
two men to finish a tantrum in her workspace — and, when she loses, going
flat and quiet rather than fighting. Where copies burn, notice the brazier
coals and the smell of it. On one route, Kesharat warriors met on the road
back, looking for Ossal.

## Social / tonal
Cold and procedural — the bleak ending. The relay stays open and the cost is
Devra: a careful, accurate woman broken into compliance, kept at a post she no
longer believes in, doing good work for a system she no longer trusts. The
register is transactional and a little shameful; Ossal is not vindicated so
much as obeyed, and his eventual inspection report will be accurate in every
particular and false in every way that matters. No supernatural note belongs
here — the horror is entirely bureaucratic and human, the quiet violence of
keeping a question closed. Keep the PC's actions plain and physical; let the
chill come from what is being preserved and at whose expense.

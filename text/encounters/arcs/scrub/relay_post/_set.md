# Set — The Relay Post

First draft. `[?]` = open question; `GAP:` = brief leaves to writer.

## Location

A Kesharat relay post (a small communications station) standing alone on the
open mesa, Scrub Tier 2. A low stone building: one main room, a back
storeroom, and a signaling apparatus on the roof. It is isolated — no
settlement at hand, just flat rock, scrub brush, and the bones of an old fence
— which is why the night belongs to the three people inside it and no one
else. Aldgate, the nearest non-Kesharat settlement, lies west; the regional
capital and the settlements the relay serves are all off the map.

- **Time of day / the clock:** the PC is caught on the mesa road by a dust
  storm at the end of a day's travel and driven to shelter. The storm lasts the
  whole night and is the arc's clock (see `_color`, "the storm as the clock"):
  evening (arrival, the debate) → night (everyone settles, the storeroom door
  shut) → midnight (the two private asks) → dawn (the storm cleared, the
  ending). Nobody travels until it clears; that is what boxes the cast in
  together.

## The building

- **The main room.** A desk against one wall holds the wire receiver — a small
  brass instrument in a cradle (the PC has no proper word for it) — and neat
  filed stacks of carbon transcriptions. A brazier with a teapot is the room's
  one source of warmth. A stool by the brazier (where Devra sits the PC down),
  a chair against the wall (where the PC dozes under a borrowed wool blanket), a
  high window that shows the first gray of dawn. The front door must be forced
  shut against the storm and latched; there is a back door with a bolt, which
  Ossal comes in by.
- **The back storeroom.** A small room off the main one with a closing door:
  a cot (kept for transient Kesharat personnel on inspection rounds), a lamp,
  and a stack of supply crates. Its closed door is the privacy mechanism for
  the midnight Ossal conversation — Devra cannot hear what is said in there.
  Ossal is offered the cot and accepts it; the twist is that he does not sleep.
- **The roof apparatus.** The signaling rig, made useless by the storm. The
  post can receive down the wire but cannot signal out — it is cut off for the
  night.

## Key objects

- **The wire receiver.** The arc's spine object (see `_color`, "the wire in its
  cradle"). Mostly silent: it ticks once and stops. Its one departure from that
  rule is the climax of the Dawn-Devra ending, when it comes alive in a long
  sequence neither survivor understands.
- **The transcriptions / the official file.** Stacks of carbon sheets, each
  carrying the standard wire header from the regional office, procedurally
  perfect and (in the anomalous ones) describing impossible things. The file is
  the public record; everything in it is "correct."
- **Devra's annotation log.** Her private second notebook — not the official
  file — in which she logs the messages that don't make sense and her attempts
  to match them to real places, materials, and people. It is what she shows the
  PC at the brazier on arrival.
- **The duplicate copies.** Actual duplicates of the original anomalous
  transcriptions, in a wax-paper sleeve, hidden in Devra's pack (under a folded
  shirt / under her bedroll). The physical evidence both help-Ossal routes
  circle: confiscated in the confront ending, lifted by a scheme or burned in
  the brazier in the take-the-copies ending. The PC only knows the sleeve
  exists if they heard Devra out at midnight (`relay_post.kept_copies`); absent
  that, the help-Ossal route is a fruitless search instead.
- **The predicted-arrival notice.** A routine maintenance message bearing this
  post's coordinates, timestamped before the PC arrived, with no origin header
  (see `_color` callback). Found on the re-read of the file in the evening hub.
- **The guild letter.** What the PC writes for Devra on the help-Devra route:
  guild form, a clean sheet from her stack, sealed with wax melted from the
  desk lamp. Five minutes' work; her passage into Aldgate.
- **The brazier.** Warmth and tea through most of the arc; in the failed
  take-the-copies scheme it is where Devra burns the duplicates, and the room
  fills with the smell of burning paper.

## Staging / privacy (so downstream keeps it plausible)

Three people, one stone room, a storm outside. The brief stages the two
private midnight conversations carefully and the prose must keep that:
- **Ossal's ask** happens behind the closed storeroom door (he beckons the PC
  in, stands with his back to it); Devra cannot hear.
- **Devra's ask** happens in the main room, low, while Ossal is supposedly
  asleep in the storeroom; she speaks quietly and glances at his door.
- **Why Devra is awake at midnight:** she keeps the night shift — the regional
  office runs three hours ahead and most of the strange messages have come in
  late. This is established before the night scene, not assumed.

## Time / clock specifics

- **The storm:** present through the night; drops to a low wind by the small
  hours; cleared to gray-pink first light by dawn, leaving the pennant torn
  half off its pole.
- **The night's shape:** evening debate → Devra offers Ossal the storeroom cot
  and takes the desk for the night shift, the PC the wall chair → midnight
  asks → dawn. The transits carry the hours that pass (the PC dozing in pieces,
  the lamp burning down) so no scene jump-cuts across the gap.

## What the set does NOT contain

Calling out absences so downstream does not invent them:

- **No combat, no `.fight`, no monster, no guard.** The only mechanical check
  in the whole arc is the single Cunning picker in the take-the-copies ending.
  Devra is an operator, not a fighter, and does not physically resist; stage no
  violence, no struggle, no chase.
- **No crystals, no cave, no formations, no glow.** This arc shares only the
  Lattice with the rest of the scrub canon, and the Lattice here manifests
  *only* through administration — the anomalous orders, the predicted-arrival
  message, and the wire coming alive once at the end. Keep the villa's and
  signal_array's crystal/pylon furniture entirely out of it.
- **No active supernatural set-piece.** Nothing glows, hums, or moves wrong.
  The receiver is an ordinary-looking brass instrument; the dread is that the
  *paperwork* is wrong, not the room.
- **No way to signal out or call for help.** The storm has killed the roof
  apparatus; the post can only receive. Devra cannot summon anyone and Ossal
  cannot work the wire at all. The three of them are on their own until dawn.
- **No fourth person inside.** Only the PC, Devra, and Ossal are in the
  building. The Kesharat warriors appear once, off the post, on the road out of
  the confront-openly ending; otherwise the patrol is an off-screen threat.
- GAP: the workings of the "wire" are deliberately vague — the PC has no frame
  for the technology. Render it in renaissance-era terms (a brass key, a
  cradle, a tick) and do not over-explain how Kesharat communications function.

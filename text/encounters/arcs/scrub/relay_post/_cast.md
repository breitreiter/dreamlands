# Cast — The Relay Post

First draft. Coverage over polish. `[?]` marks an open question;
`GAP:` marks something the brief leaves to the writer.

The defining fact of this cast: two living people hold opposed certainties in
one stone room, and both are honest. Unlike a corpse-and-journal arc, the
horror here is carried by people present to argue with — Devra has evidence
and no framework, Ossal has a framework and meets the evidence selectively —
and by the paperwork between them. The PC is a guild outsider whose standing
(a sealed letter) is the lever that can tip the night one way or the other.
Neither NPC is a villain; the arc gives the PC no clean enemy.

## Devra — the relay operator

The arc's evidence, and its conscience. Mid-thirties, sharp-featured,
ink-stained fingers, the focused attention of a person whose whole job is to
listen carefully and write down exactly what she hears. Four years running
this post, alone tonight. Good at the work and proud of her accuracy. Beneath
the competence, quietly terrified — and over the evening the PC watches the
fear surface.

- **Role:** Kesharat relay operator. Messages arrive down the wire from the
  regional capital; she transcribes them; runners carry the transcriptions to
  the settlements downstream. She files what comes, faithfully, because that
  is the job — including the orders that make no sense. She has also begun
  keeping a second notebook (her private annotation log) and hidden duplicate
  copies of the anomalous transcriptions in her pack.
- **Knows:** the wire and the post cold; which recent orders don't add up and
  why (a resonance platform at empty coordinates, four hundred units of a
  material no catalog lists, a transfer for an ID matching no format); that one
  message carried this building's coordinates, timestamped before the PC
  arrived, with no origin header.
- **Cannot:** prove her theory or name what is behind it. She has the
  evidence and no framework for it. She is not a fighter — an operator, not a
  guard; when she loses, she does not struggle, she goes flat and quiet.
- **Wants:** out. To leave at first light for Aldgate (the nearest non-Kesharat
  settlement, west), not back to the capital. A guild letter of introduction so
  she crosses the gate as something other than a deserter, and someone outside
  the Kesharat system to evaluate her copies clean. She is not asking the PC to
  endorse her theory — only for a piece of paper.
- **Voice:** precise, a careful listener's diction. Early, a slightly manic
  warmth from too much solitude — she talks too much, pours tea, shows the PC
  her log. At midnight, low and quick, glancing at the storeroom door. When
  confronted, she argues passionately and logically, citation by citation from
  her log, until it is clear the PC will not move — and then she stops.
- GAP: surname, origin, family all unstated in the brief. Default: leave her
  Devra, no surname; a Kesharat townswoman, not Tashkari.

## Ossal — the inspector (the believer)

The arc's framework, and its quiet antagonist. Old — seventies, perhaps
eighties — with the careful, energy-conserving movements of a man rationing
himself. A Kesharat administrative inspector on a circuit of remote posts,
weeks on the mesa, tired. Beneath the fatigue, a true believer, fifty years
in the service.

- **Role:** Kesharat administrative inspector — checks that procedures are
  followed and equipment maintained. Off the post walking the signal apparatus
  when the PC arrives; comes in the back door out of the storm. He makes the
  case for the Lattice: the Administration is its instrument, it perceives
  connections humans cannot, its directives only look arbitrary because human
  understanding is small. He states all of this calmly, reasonably, without
  condescension — as plainly as a supply schedule. He believes it because the
  alternative (that the system he served for fifty years is being hijacked)
  is not a thing a person accepts easily.
- **Knows:** Kesharat procedure cold; the theology, which he holds as plain
  secular fact. The stakes if the relay goes dark: the settlements downstream
  lose supply orders, medical requests, patrol coordination.
- **Cannot:** operate the wire. He is an inspector, not an operator — this is
  load-bearing: in the search-the-post ending he cannot even tell the PC what
  evidence would prove what, because he cannot work the receiver to confirm
  anything from the capital. And he cannot accept Devra's conclusion; detaining
  her is partly about keeping the question closed.
- **Wants:** the relay kept open and Devra detained until a patrol arrives —
  her copies confiscated, the post secured, a replacement operator sent. At
  midnight he frames this in entirely secular terms (a disloyal employee, the
  settlements' dependence) and never mentions the Lattice; he also insinuates
  that Devra is unwell, isolation-addled, a reputational risk to the Kesharat.
  He is right about the consequences and afraid underneath of what it means if
  she is right.
- **Voice:** measured, the patience of a priest hearing a doubting
  parishioner; chooses words with the precision of a man who has written
  reports his whole life. In the Dawn-Devra rupture (she leaves) he breaks —
  cursing her by name, family, and office, swearing patrols and chains, turning
  insinuation and a warning about the Trade Guild on the PC.
- GAP: full name and exact rank unstated. Default: Ossal, no surname; an
  inspector of middling seniority on a long circuit.

## The PC

A Traders Guild member (signet ring, travels alone; never named), sheltering
from a dust storm. Guild standing is the lever the whole night turns on: a
sealed guild letter can get Devra into Aldgate, which is why both NPCs court
the PC. Renaissance-era eye — no proper word for the wire apparatus, no frame
for the Lattice; reads the wire messages as strange paperwork and the two NPCs
as a merchant reads two counterparties who have argued before. The PC arrives
with whatever they happened to be carrying; assume nothing (no mount, no
companion, no special kit) per the canonical NO GAME STATE rule.

## NPCs implied but not present (do not stage)

- **The Kesharat patrol / the two warriors.** Mostly an off-screen threat —
  the patrol Ossal swears he will summon. The one time they appear on screen:
  the confront-openly ending, where the PC meets a pair of Kesharat warriors on
  the road out, looking for Ossal, and exchanges pleasantries / directs them to
  the post. Render per `text/lore/scrub.md` (modern bureaucratic Kesharat
  soldiery): polite, procedural, unhurried. Keep them otherwise off-screen.
- **The Lattice.** Not a character. The arc's premise is that it communicates
  by hijacking human systems — it does not issue pronouncements, it sends
  memos. It never speaks except as the wire coming alive at the very end (Dawn
  Devra), and its only fingerprints are that one beat and the message that
  predicted the arrival. Do not personify it.
- **The regional capital / regional office.** The supposed source of every
  transcription, and an abstraction. The horror is precisely that some messages
  did not come from it. Never seen.
- **The settlements downstream.** Named only as stakes (the people who lose the
  relay if it goes dark — supply orders, medical requests, patrol coordination).
  Never seen.
- **Aldgate / its guild chapter.** Devra's destination and the addressee of the
  letter the PC may write. Off-screen; the arc never goes there.

# Lens — Midnight (The Two Requests)

How the PC sees this scene. Steering for color: whose eye, what to notice,
in what key. Stance, not facts.

## Perceptual
Deep night in the post: lamp-light, the storm dropped to a low wind, the wire
silent in its cradle, the storeroom door closed. The eye is close and quiet,
tuned to whispered conversation and to who is awake and who is not. Read Devra
when she crosses the floor and crouches at the chair — speaking low so as not
to wake the old man, frightened and precise, asking for a single sheet of
paper with a guild seal on it and nothing more. Read Ossal in the storeroom
doorway, fully dressed and never asleep, choosing his words with the care of a
man who has written reports his whole life, making an entirely secular case: a
disloyal employee, unauthorized copies, the settlements downstream that lose
their relay if she runs. Notice the wax-paper sleeve of duplicate
transcriptions as the physical thing both pleas circle. Underneath both
people, fear: Devra of the system she serves, Ossal of what it means if she is
right. Render that fear as observable behavior, not as stated interior.

## Social / tonal
Hushed and conspiratorial, the moral weight of the arc landing here. Two
private pleas delivered in a one-room building, staged so that neither speaker
can hear the other — the storeroom door, the low voices, the careful spacing
of the hours. The horror now lives in the *choice*, not in the dark: both
asks are reasonable, both people are sympathetic, and neither is a villain.
The register is human strain under a clock ticking toward dawn. Keep the
cosmic note offstage entirely; this scene is about two frightened people and
the small, heavy thing the PC is being asked to do. The wire stays silent —
its quiet is part of the pressure.

# Lens — Completion (the pylon goes live)

How the PC sees this scene. Steering for color — whose eye, what to notice, in
what key. Stance, not facts.

## Perceptual
A traveller watching a well-run crew close out a job: cables checked one by one,
the top piece fitted, the kit stowed, the sequence read off a worn laminated
card, the last bolt tight, the key turned. Then the click and the cycling-up
inside the housing. The eye is forced outward onto the crew and the machine —
what their hands and faces do as they go absent, the closed and latched housing
at the base, the cables, the light, the sound.

## Social / tonal
Wonder to dread to horror, the peak of the arc — a beat that already asserts the
wrong, so color's job is to give it observable flesh, not to manufacture more.

The horror here happens inside the PC's head, and that interior belongs to the
writer, NOT to color. Do not render the signal's content: no blueprints,
schematics, coordinates, part numbers, scheduling margins, no "a weight behind
the eyes," no "a hum at the back of the skull." Those are the writer's. Color
records only what a lens or a microphone catches in the room — the click, the
cycling-up, the crew present in body and gone behind the eyes, the housing, the
hands, the cables. And do not pile near-identical lines on the crew leaving:
one or two observed details of their departure is enough, not a dozen.

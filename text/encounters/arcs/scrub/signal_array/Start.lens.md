# Lens — The Pylon Crew (Arrival)

How the PC sees this scene. Steering for color — whose eye, what to notice, in
what key. Stance, not facts.

## Perceptual
A renaissance-era traveller on the mesa road at the end of a hot afternoon,
coming on a Kesharat work crew the way you come on any roadside labour: the
half-built metal tower first (more worked iron standing open on the road than a
village smith sees in a year, struts and guy-cables, nothing a mason would
recognise), then the four men at the housing at its base. The eye is a
stranger's and an obstacle's — the heat still coming off the rock, the smell of
hot metal when the wind gusts, the cable run you are waved around. Name the
machinery in your own words; you have no word for what it is.

## Social / tonal
An opening: color's job is baseline normal. Mostly aliveness and grounding — the
tower, the heat, the work going on without you. The crew routes you like a
hazard, not a guest. The off-key register is a rare seam caught while you wait,
and only what the beats name: a clan band at a rolled cuff, the wrong sunburn
above a collar, the fourth man buttoned up and too still. Then the bell, and the
warmth arrives all at once. Do not argue the wrongness; record the seam and move
on.

# The Signal Array (Scrub Tier 2)

A Kesharat infrastructure crew erecting a communications pylon on the mesa
road. They are near the end of a long job and happy to take a break when the
PC arrives. They are friendly, competent, proud of their work. Over the course
of a siesta, it becomes clear that each of them has a gap — a stretch of the
journey they cannot account for, plastered over with plausible narrative they
do not scrutinize.

**Deeper concept:** The Lattice does not issue commands. It offers employment.
When the pylon activates, it floods nearby minds with blueprints, schematics,
coordinates, part numbers — the full weight of meaningful purposeful work. The
horror is not that it destroys you. It is that it hires you. Resistance is not
fighting off an attack. It is declining a job that, in the moment, makes a
kind of sense.

**Hook:** The PC encounters a cheerful work crew on siesta, a few hours from
completing their job. The gaps in their stories are visible to the PC. They
are not visible to the crew.

**Clutch:** The pylon activates. The recruitment signal hits. The PC resists.
In the thin window before the crew re-aligns, the PC can move on the activator
housing — or not.

# Scene Sketch

## Opening

The PC comes upon the crew on the mesa road: four workers and a pile of
equipment around a half-erected pylon — a lattice structure, five meters tall,
with a metal housing at the base and cable runs disappearing into the rock. It
is clearly Kesharat infrastructure. Communications relay, by the look of it.

The foreman calls a siesta when the PC arrives. They have been at this since
dawn. Two hours of work remain, and the cool of late afternoon is a better
time to finish. They make room, share tea, and talk. They are good company.

The second thing the PC notices, after "good company," is that this crew has
no business being a crew. The foreman is Kesharat infrastructure, which makes
sense. The young man next to him is wearing a Reshîd clan band on his wrist
and has the bearing of someone who grew up working rope and stone — not
Kesharat, and given recent census-house business, not someone the Reshîd would
lend to a Kesharat project. The woman across from him is clearly not from the
scrub at all: wrong clothes for the climate, accent from somewhere weeks away
in any direction. The fourth man the PC may have encountered before, in a
different context entirely, doing something else — he does not appear to
recognize the PC, and his account of where he has been lately will not match
what the PC knows of him.

These four are sharing tea and ribbing each other about the morning's work
with the easy familiarity of people who have been traveling together for
weeks. Nobody has mentioned the obvious. Possibly nobody has noticed.

## The Crew

**Veran** is the foreman — mid-fifties, weathered, a career Kesharat
infrastructure worker who takes visible pride in his crew's precision. His gap
is spatial. He describes the route from the last site to this one in confident
detail, and the route does not cohere: it passes through terrain that would add
a week to the journey, told in the time the direct road would take. He does not
notice. If the PC presses, he has a satisfying explanation that also does not
quite add up, and he seems mildly puzzled that the question is being asked at
all.

**Baret** has a Reshîd clan band and the hands of someone who grew up
working stone. He is friendly and easy, talking about his family constantly —
his mother, his sisters, a nephew born while he has been on the road. He was
supposed to be home for the birth. He knows he is overdue. He has a clear
expectation that he will be home after this job, just this one more. He has
said this before; he knows he has said this before and treats it almost as a
joke on himself. He has no apparent awareness that working a Kesharat
infrastructure job is something a Reshîd elder's son would need to explain.
If asked, he has an explanation. The explanation is fine. It does not hold up
to the second question.

**Sevet** is clearly not from anywhere near the scrub: wrong clothes, wrong
accent, wrong assumptions about the weather. She has the most complete
explanation of what the pylons are for — communications infrastructure for
remote settlements, improving administrative connectivity across the mesa. Her
account is detailed and delivered with the fluency of someone who genuinely
understands the work. The errors are small — technical details that do not
match how any real communications system functions — and stated with enough
confidence that challenging them feels pedantic. She is not lying. She knows
this. She just knows it wrong. She does not have an account of how she came to
be doing infrastructure work in a biome she has clearly never been in before.
She has a feeling of how, which is not the same thing.

**The fourth worker** is for the writer to fit to the PC's history: someone
from elsewhere in the arc roster, a named NPC the PC has plausibly met, now
here, doing this, with no apparent memory of the previous encounter or a
confused and slightly wrong account of it. The dissonance should be
proportional to how incompatible their previous context was with "Kesharat
pylon crew." A census clerk, a relay operator, a clan elder's representative —
whoever would be most wrong. If the PC has not met anyone suitable, he is
simply a deserter from a Kesharat garrison unit, which is a crime, and Veran
does not appear to know or care.

## The Siesta

The PC can speak with any or all of them. Veran is approachable if a little
formal; Baret is easy company; Sevet is the most willing to discuss the
technical side of the work; Chorik is the quietest and, to the attentive PC,
the closest to the surface of something. Not because he suspects anything —
because the gaps sit shallower in him than in the others.

None of them are performing. The gaps are how memory works when you have been
inside the signal and outside it enough times. You fill in what is missing with
what makes sense. The filling-in is automatic and feels like remembering.

For the PC who has been to the relay post: the anomalous wire traffic in
Devra's files lives here. The resonance platform at empty-desert coordinates.
The material called "aligned calcium." The employee with the malformed
identification number. These are pylon sites, pylon components, pylon crew
designations. The messages that arrived through the wire with no origin header
were not communications passing through the relay. They were construction
coordination. The system was scheduling this.

## Completion

The crew finishes in the late afternoon. They are efficient. The foreman
checks cable connections and signs off. Chorik fits the housing around the
base. Sevet runs the activation sequence from a laminated card. A quiet click,
something cycling up inside the housing, and the pylon is live.

The weight is immediate. Not pain — purpose. Blueprints unroll in the PC's
mind. Schematics with part numbers for components they have never encountered
but suddenly understand the function of. Coordinates for the next site.
Material quantities. The full weight of a job that is organized and waiting and
makes complete sense.

The PC resists. This is not a question of whether they make it through — they
do. It is a question of what they hold onto while they push it back, and how
far in the recruitment gets before they close it out. A bad roll means catching
more of it before the grip firms: more of the blueprints, more of the
coordinates, a clearer picture of what the mesh of nodes across the scrub is
building toward.

The PC surfaces. The crew is already somewhere else behind the eyes.

## The Window

For a few seconds the crew is mid-transition: present in body, absent in the
way that matters, not yet coordinated. The activator housing is at the base of
the pylon. The PC has just felt exactly what it does.

This is the window. It will not stay open.

## The Choice

### Act

The PC moves on the housing. The crew turns — not with aggression, exactly,
but with the focused purposefulness of people protecting equipment. They do not
fight like soldiers. They fight like workers who have a job and the PC is in
the way of it.

A Cunning check: find the weak point in the housing and break it before the
crew is fully coordinated. If it succeeds, the activator cracks. The signal
cuts. If it fails, Veran gets a hand on the PC first, and it becomes a Combat
check to disengage before the others close.

Either way, once the housing breaks, the signal is gone.

### Walk Away

The PC lets the window close. The crew finishes their transition. They collect
their tools with the synchronized economy of people who know exactly where they
are going and why. The pylon hums behind them as they head down the road. The
PC is alone on the mesa with it.

## Aftermath: Act

The crew's gap fills in the way it always does, automatically, with what makes
sense.

A merchant, they piece together. Came along during the siesta, seemed friendly
enough. Clearly something wrong with them — heat sickness, maybe, or something
they ate out here. Went at the housing without warning. Baret has bruised ribs
from trying to pull them off. Nothing broken. The housing is wrecked and they
need a new activator unit, which means three days back to the depot, which
Veran is going to have strong feelings about when the adrenaline settles.

What was with that weirdo.

Before the narrative fully sets, the PC has a window for a Negotiation check.
The target is not "convince them of the Lattice" — that is not reachable. The
target is one crack in the plaster, wide enough for one of them to look
through. If it succeeds, Chorik goes quiet. He looks at the PC. He looks at
his hands. Veran calls his name and he goes.

The PC walks away not knowing if anything landed.

### Walk Away

The PC walks. Behind them the pylon hums. Ahead: the mesa road, the
settlements downstream, Devra's wire with its messages that do not come from
the capital, the next site with coordinates the PC now knows.

None of that is the PC's problem. The despair is just the weight of having
felt what the job offer felt like and knowing the crew took it, and knowing the
depot is three weeks from here in the other direction and there are more sites
after this one.

# Cast — The Signal Array

First draft. Coverage over polish. `[?]` marks an open question;
`GAP:` marks something the brief leaves to the writer.

## Veran — foreman

- **Role:** Kesharat infrastructure foreman. Onsite authority.
  Signs off completion.
- **Age / appearance:** mid-fifties. Weathered. Career fieldworker
  build — forearms, sun-creased neck, hands that have set anchor
  bolts for thirty years.
- **Uniform read:** wears it correctly, fully kitted, looks born
  to it. Tunic buttoned to the throat. Belt sits right, bell
  clipped where a bell should clip. Of the four, the only one
  whose uniform reads as *his.*
- **Knows:**
  - Pylon assembly procedure cold. Can walk the steps without the
    laminated card.
  - The schedule. Winds the interval bell each shift.
  - The route from the last site to here (he believes; see gap).
- **Cannot:**
  - Notice that his account of the route adds a week the schedule
    didn't have.
  - Notice that his crew composition is impossible.
  - Recognize the recruitment signal as a recruitment signal,
    before, during, or after.
- **Wants:** finish the job well. Crew home safe. Next site.
- **Voice:** formal, even with strangers. Calls the crew by name.
  Speaks of the work in the third person — "the pylon," "the
  housing," not "ours." Mild, careful, proud. Says "the bell"
  and "interval's up" the way a man says "lunch."
- **Carries the interval bell** on his belt. Does not look at
  it before it rings. Does not look at it after.
- **Pre-bell behavior:** routes the PC around the work site
  with utilitarian instructions, no warmth, no rudeness. The PC
  is a hazard, not a guest.
- **Post-bell behavior:** turns, smiles, makes room, asks the
  PC's name. The transition is total and he does not
  acknowledge it.
- **Gap (the Lattice signature):** **spatial.** Route from last
  site to this one. Confident details, geometry doesn't close.
  Second question surfaces a satisfying second explanation that
  also doesn't close. Mild puzzlement that the question is being
  asked.

## Baret — Reshîd, on the crew

- **Role:** crew labor. Stonework, rigging, anchoring.
- **Age / appearance:** late twenties [?]. Hands of someone
  who grew up working stone. Easy posture.
- **Uniform read:** standard issue, cuffs rolled to the elbow.
  The roll exposes the Reshîd clan band at his wrist — visible
  whenever he reaches for the kettle or sets a tool down. The
  tunic fits him broad through the shoulders, not quite right.
  Wears it like borrowed work clothes: practical, no pride.
- **Knows:**
  - His family — mother, sisters, nephew born while he's been
    away. Talks about them constantly.
  - That he is overdue home. Treats it as a joke on himself.
  - Stonework, rope, anchor sets. Real skill, recognizable to a
    PC with bushcraft.
- **Cannot:**
  - Account for being on a Kesharat infrastructure crew. A
    Reshîd elder's son working for Kesharat is, in the current
    political climate, something that would need explaining.
    Baret has an explanation. It survives one question, not two.
  - See that "just this one more job" is a phrase he has said
    before, more than once, and that he can hear himself saying
    it. (He hears it. He doesn't follow the thread.)
- **Wants:** to go home. Means it. Has meant it for a long time.
- **Voice:** warm. Easy. Family talk pours out. Code-switches
  into Reshîd cadence when relaxed; standard scrub register with
  the foreman.
- **Pre-bell:** working. Quiet, focused, doesn't look up.
  Sets anchor bolts the way someone sets anchor bolts.
- **Post-bell:** lays out the rest-circle mat without being
  asked. Has the kettle on before Veran sits down. Talks the
  whole time.
- **Gap:** **relational / temporal.** Why he is here, working
  for them. How long he has actually been away. The nephew's
  age, if pressed against dates, doesn't line up.
- **Cross-arc note:** Reshîd clan figures elsewhere in scrub arc
  roster — `[?]` check `census_house` (deleted) and current
  `the_villa` for Reshîd characters that should know Baret or
  his family. GAP: pick a small linking detail that lands if PC
  has met them, lands harmlessly if not.

## Richard — pig farmer, technical

- **Role:** crew technician. Runs the activation sequence.
- **Age / appearance:** forties [?]. Built like a man who lifts
  hogs.
- **Uniform read:** standard issue. Fills it heavy through the
  chest and belly. Collar left unbuttoned and the neck cloth
  loose — and above the collar line, the back of his neck
  shows the wrong *kind* of sunburn: grassland sun, the kind
  you get standing in an open pasture, not the dry-mesa burn
  the rest of the crew is wearing. The cuffs are rolled. The
  hands inside them are a farmer's, not a fieldworker's.
- **Origin:** the rich grasslands near Aldgate. He has a farm.
  He has a prize pig.
- **Knows:**
  - The pig. Name, age [?], temperament, what she eats, what
    she will not eat, what her ribbons look like when she has
    won them.
  - His brother. The brother is seeing to the pig in his
    absence. Richard has *opinions* about the brother's
    husbandry. None of them are flattering.
  - The harvest festival calendar. Reckons (correctly) that
    it's about that time. Wonders if the PC has been by the
    farm. Asks. Means it.
  - The pylon. Fluent technical patter — propagation,
    scheduling, tolerances, the activation sequence cold. Reads
    it from the laminated card, but reads it the way a man
    reads a page he has written himself.
  - The errors are small and confident. Real communications
    relays don't work the way he describes. Challenging him
    feels pedantic.
- **Cannot:**
  - Account for how a pig farmer from Aldgate came to be doing
    scrub infrastructure work. He has a *feeling* of how. Not
    a memory.
  - See the seam between the coarse plain-spoken farmer and the
    precise technical voice. They are both his. He moves
    between them without noticing.
- **Wants:** the pig to be all right. The job done well. To go
  home before the festival, if the work allows.
- **Voice:** plain-spoken and coarse — the farmer register —
  except on technical matters, where he is astute, precise,
  and slightly clinical. The shift is audible mid-sentence and
  he does not notice it.
- **Pre-bell:** at the housing, hands deep in the cable bundle,
  curt. Will tell the PC to stand clear and not look up while
  he says it.
- **Post-bell:** drops the bundle, sits down heavy, asks the
  PC where they've come from, asks about the farm, asks about
  the pig.
- **Gap:** **biographical / vocational.** No account of how a
  grassland pig farmer ended up running activation sequences
  on a mesa. The technical fluency is correct-shaped, wrong-
  detailed. The farmer is real. The technician is real. They
  cannot both be true.

## Chorik — the fourth

- **Role:** crew labor. Fits the housing during completion.
- **Age / appearance:** GAP — the brief allows fitting to the
  PC's history. Default: late twenties, scrub features,
  unremarkable. Whatever he is, he does not match the other
  three the way they match each other.
- **Uniform read:** the most correct of the four. Buttoned
  throat to sternum exactly, neck cloth folded crisp, cuffs
  down. Hat where a hat should be. Visually he is the most
  *fitted.* The wrongness is not in the uniform — it is in
  how still he holds himself inside it. He does not sweat
  through it the way the others do. He does not adjust it.
  He occupies the uniform; the uniform does not occupy him.
- **Knows:**
  - The housing fit, the cable runs. Competent, quiet hands.
- **Cannot:**
  - Tell a story about himself. Declines, deflects, changes
    the subject. Not hostile — uncomfortable.
  - Match the easy familiarity the others extend him.
- **Wants:** to not be looked at. To finish the job. To not be
  *known.* He is the only crew member who carries a low-grade
  anxiety about his presence here, even though he cannot name
  what is wrong.
- **Voice:** quiet. Short sentences. Often a beat behind the
  laughter. Does not initiate. When the others rib him he
  smiles thinly and lets the moment pass.
- **Pre-bell:** working alongside the others, indistinguishable
  in pace, no different from them. The discomfort is not
  visible while the work is on.
- **Post-bell:** the only one whose flip is incomplete. He sits
  where they put him. He drinks the tea they pour. The merry
  band closes around him and he does not close back.
- **Gap:** **social.** The other three treat Chorik as one of
  their merry band — old jokes, shared shorthand, "remember
  when Chorik did the thing with the —" stories that Chorik
  conspicuously does not corroborate. The others vocally
  patch over his discomfort with easy lies and excuses on his
  behalf:
    - "He's been quiet since the last site, don't mind him."
    - "Chorik's just tired, aren't you Chorik."
    - "Don't ask him about that, he doesn't like to talk about
      it." (Said about a thing the speaker has just invented.)
  Chorik does not contradict the lies. He does not confirm
  them either. He sits inside them.
- **Authoring note:** Chorik is the PC's leverage in the
  Negotiation-aftermath beat. He is the one who, if the
  Negotiation check lands, goes quiet and looks at his hands.
  The discomfort is the seam; the others' patching is the
  plaster. Build him with both from the start.

## NPCs implied but not present

- **The Lattice.** Not a character. A scheduling presence.
  Never speaks. The recruitment signal is its only direct
  contact with the PC. The crew is its handiwork, not its
  voice.
- **The previous-site crew.** Mentioned by Veran. Not seen.
  GAP: do we want them named, or do they stay an abstraction?
  Default: abstraction. The horror is that "the previous site"
  exists, not who was there.
- **Devra (from relay_post).** Off-screen. Her wire traffic is
  the PC's prior context that lets the pylon-completion beat
  hit harder. No appearance here.

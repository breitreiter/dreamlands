# Set — The Signal Array

First draft. `[?]` = open question; `GAP:` = brief leaves to writer.

## Location

The mesa road, late afternoon. Tier 2 scrub. A stretch where the
road runs along an exposed shelf with a broken horizon line —
visible distance in three directions, the mesa wall climbing on
the fourth. The site was chosen for line-of-sight, not shelter.

- **Sun:** lowering, west. Heat still in the rock; the air
  starting to slacken. The bell tracks with the worst
  hour passing.
- **Wind:** intermittent. Carries the smell of hot metal off the
  pylon when it gusts.
- **Ground:** rock and scree. Anchor bolts have been set into
  the bedrock. Cable runs disappear into a cut in the rock
  toward the mesa — GAP: where the cables actually go is left
  unanswered in the brief. They go *somewhere.* The PC does not
  follow them in this arc.
- **Other traffic:** none. The mesa road is quiet at this hour.

## The pylon (half-erected at arrival, complete by activation)

- **Shape:** lattice tower, ~5m tall when complete. Open
  framework, metal struts, guyed with cable.
- **State at arrival:** main frame up, top cross-piece not yet
  fitted, housing at base open. Tools laid out around it.
- **State at completion:** top fitted, housing closed,
  activation card in Richard's hands.
- **Activator housing:** metal box at the base of the pylon,
  bolted to the bedrock. Cables enter from below. After
  completion: closed, latched, panel readable but not opened
  without effort. **This is the target in the Act branch.**

## Crew uniform

Kesharat infrastructure field issue. All four wear it. Cut for
the arid climate, period-appropriate (circa-1800s functional —
no synthetics, no reflective anything, no jumpsuits):

- **Trousers:** unbleached canvas, dyed dun, loose through the
  thigh, cuffed at the boot.
- **Tunic:** long-sleeved, same dun canvas, button placket from
  throat to sternum, cuffs that can be rolled. Cut roomy for
  ventilation.
- **Belt:** broad oiled leather. Brass buckle. Veran's bell
  clips to this.
- **Neck cloth:** white linen, folded once and tucked into the
  collar against the sun.
- **Hat:** broad-brimmed, felt or stiffened straw, with a
  kerchief tied beneath for the back of the neck. GAP: pick
  one (felt for foreman? straw for labor? or all the same).
- **Insignia:** small brass button at the throat stamped with
  the Kesharat infrastructure mark. The only ornament. GAP:
  what the mark looks like — leave to the writer / art pass.
- **Boots:** standard work boot. Leather. Ankle-height. GAP.

**What the PC reads.** Uniforms in the PC's experience are
Imperial Legion (red coat, plate or mail), city guard (tabard
over chain), or Imperial functionaries (dark frock coat, brass
buttons, official seal). This is none of those — closer to a
railway-crew or surveyor's kit than to any soldier or clerk —
but the brass button at the throat marks it unmistakably as
*issued.* Workers in the PC's world generally wear what they
scrounge from home. Kesharat workers do not.

The horror cue is that the uniform fits the crew. All four of
them. Including the ones it shouldn't fit.

## Crew equipment

- Tool roll laid out near the pylon. Wrenches, a torque bar,

  not pleasant — a single hard clap of sound and a sustained
  buzz. Kesharat field-issue.
- A tea kit stowed in Veran's pack until the bell rings, then
  produced and set out. Small pot, four cups, a kettle.
- Bedrolls and packs stacked off to one side. Four sets.
- The laminated activation card — Richard's. Folded twice,
  edges worn. He knows where it is in his pack without looking.

## The rest interval (and the flip)

Kesharat infrastructure crews work on scheduled intervals: a
stretch of intensive work, then a fixed rest period, then
back. The bell tracks it. When the bell rings, the rest
interval begins. The crew does not decide to rest. They are
on rest. The administrative term is **rest interval**; the
crew says "the bell," "interval's up," "got another two on
the clock."

The rest circle: a flat patch of rock about ten meters from
the pylon, set up by Baret in under a minute once the bell
goes — bedroll mat laid out, kit produced, kettle on. Four
crew ring around it. Veran sits a little upslope — foreman's
habit. Baret next to him. Richard across from Baret, heavy in
his seat. Chorik off-axis, the one whose feet are not quite
tucked in. There is room for a fifth.

**The flip.** Before the bell, the crew is at work and the PC
is an obstacle. Utilitarian register: "step wide of the cable
run, friend," "mind the tools," eyes on the housing, sparse
acknowledgment. No hostility, no warmth — the PC is a hazard
to be routed around. Then the bell. Tools down mid-motion.
Postures unwind. Veran is the first to turn to the PC and now
he is smiling. Baret sets out the mat. Richard asks where the
PC has come from like he means it. The shift is total and it
takes about three seconds.

## Time beats

- **Arrival:** late afternoon. Crew mid-work, on the schedule.
  PC is an obstacle for the first beat or two.
- **The bell:** rings shortly after PC arrives. Crew flips.
  Rest interval begins. GAP: do we want the bell to ring at a
  fixed moment after arrival, or to be PC-triggered (e.g. the
  player chooses to wait nearby and it rings)? Default: fixed,
  shortly after arrival — the schedule is not on the PC.
- **Rest interval:** the length of one cup of tea, then a
  second, then a stretch. GAP: how many beats the writer wants
  here — the brief invites 3–4 separable conversations plus
  ambient.
- **The bell again:** marks the end of the rest interval. The
  crew rises. The warmth does not vanish the way it arrived —
  it tapers. GAP: do we want a second hard flip back, or a
  softer return to work? Default: softer. The flip-in is the
  signature; the flip-out is unremarkable.
- **Completion:** crew returns to the pylon, ~20 minutes of
  focused work. Top piece, housing close, cable check.
- **Activation:** the click. Cycling-up sound. Live.
- **Recruitment signal:** seconds, subjectively much longer.
- **Window:** few seconds, real time, after the PC surfaces.
- **Aftermath:** the crew's gap fills in over a minute or two.
  Either branch resolves in that minute.

## What the set does NOT contain

Calling out absences to keep downstream from inventing them:

- **No stools, chairs, or furniture.** Rock seating only.
- **No tent or shelter.** This is a scheduled rest on the work
  site, not a camp.
- **No other vehicles or pack animals.** GAP: how did the crew
  arrive with their kit? Brief leaves it. Default: pack animal
  tethered off-frame, not engaged with in this arc.
- **No weapons drawn or visible.** The crew are workers. Veran
  may have a personal knife. Nobody has a sword propped against
  a rock.
- **No second pylon visible.** The "next site" is named in
  blueprint, not seen.
- **No pig.** Richard's prize pig is at home in Aldgate. Do
  not put a pig on the mesa.

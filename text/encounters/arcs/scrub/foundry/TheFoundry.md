## Scrub Capstone: The Kesharat Manufactory

**Premise.** Suhail — one of the original warriors who unsealed the entity, exempted from Lattice control and cursed with something that passes for immortality — intends to march on a tier-3 Kesharat facility and bring it down. He invites the PC to come. He cannot say why. The `foundry.challenge` counter tracks the PC's interventions across the three factory spaces: regard extended at personal cost toward husks who cannot return it. The finale checks the count.

**Canon.** The facts the arc draws on. Surface material can be shown or said early; reserved material is deployed late, for effect, and is never exposition-dumped. The descent runs four explorations at once — the factory, the Lattice, the region's history, and Suhail's private quarrel with the thing he helped loose — and Suhail fills in his own background by pieces, on the road, as it suits him, never as a lecture.

*Suhail (surface):*
- Appears as a wild-haired, unkempt vagrant — no status, no papers, no obvious business. Yet he is permitted everywhere, always, without question. The guards refuse the PC, who by now carries a trade guild member's standing, and wave Suhail through. The contrast is a deliberate puzzle the arc never fully answers up front.
- Was one of the warriors who opened the door, long ago. When it was opened, the survivors were offered a deal: exemption from the entity's direct control, something that passes for immortality, positions of authority in the new state. Most took it. A few, finding neither option livable, refused in every sense and are gone. Suhail carries the exemption and the curse but took none of the authority — a category of one.
- The permission that follows him everywhere is not something he chose. The guards' deference is recognition, not respect.
- Playfully evasive about all of it. Deflects direct questions lightly; volunteers fragments when it amuses him.

*The entity (surface):*
- He calls it different things. The Lattice is the civic word — the one his former cohort turned into governance and philosophy. He uses older names too, from before it had a name that sounded like administration.
- The Color is its physical trace, recurring environmental motif: a purple-beyond-purple staining the deep scrub, the sky refracted into pale threads. The PC can see and feel it from Scene 1, but that it *is* the Lattice — the entity made visible, the thing watching from inside its own light — is reserved. The PC walks across the entity's substance for days without knowing it; the Control Room confession should pay this off.
- Render the Color the way relentless desert glare actually feels, not the way an eerie glow reads on a page. Everything too bright, always; nowhere to rest the eye; the after-image burned in whether the eyes are open or shut; a grinding low-grade fatigue and headache that the body blames on the sun and never gets to escape. The horror is the no-relief, not the strangeness.

*The eyes — the spine of it:* The Color in the eyes is the mark of Lattice alignment, its final form. The workers wear it overtly: brute-force control, hollowed and marked, the sort of thing the PC has likely glimpsed signs of before in Kesharat territory. Suhail wears the same mark — but he is not held that way. He is ostensibly free, and that is the horror of his case: the Lattice does not seize him by force, it keeps him by something subtler and almost respectful, a leash he can feel and name and not break. He is aware. He can do nothing. Same mark, two grips — the crude one on the husks, the courteous one on him — and it is the courtesy that infuriates him. The workers are what total absorption looks like; Suhail is what the Lattice does with someone it finds worth keeping intact. Hold the *interpretation* of this in reserve; the matching eyes are visible from Scene 3, but what they mean is earned across the descent.

*The blade:* A futuristic industrial cutting tool — a CNC mill cutter about the length of an arm, drawn out of the machine's cutting head — crudely fashioned into a weapon by a renaissance-era hand: the tang wrapped in a rag, nothing more. The contrast is the whole appeal: a precision instrument of an unimaginably advanced civilization, reduced to a thing you stab with. Color-tempered edge; its cut makes metal flare with the Color and cool to a low glow — residual phosphorescence, like friction-heat fading from bright to a smolder. Physical and temporary, not a Lady-Macbeth stain; keep it plain. The blade doesn't need to do more than this — no hidden marks, no reveal. Suhail draws it competently, running the machine's shutdown first rather than prying it loose. **Leave open how he knows the machine.** The PC has seen the Lattice imbue technical knowledge into people who never trained, so whether Suhail picked this up over a long life or was primed is a question the scene raises and never answers — quietly feeding the spine: a man this competent inside the machine may be more handled than he knows.

*The blade is also the one thing that can kill him.* Ordinary weapons do nothing to a man the deal made deathless; the Color-tempered edge is the exception — the same edge that bit the seal. This is the unspoken second meaning of *you'll rest easier with me unarmed*: he is not only disarming himself, he is handing the PC the only key to his own death. Keep the second meaning reserved — it lands at the finale, when ordinary force fails him and the blade does not. (Mechanically: the finale kill is gated on `has shimmering_blade`; a normal-weapon attempt is a separate, failing outcome.)

*Reserved — deploy late, never in Scene 1:*
- That Suhail is literally centuries old. **Deployed in the Cutting Machine space (3b)** — he says the number flatly while running the shutdown, paying off the "arithmetic doesn't sit right" tease from Scene 2.
- That the Lattice is a sentient, extradimensional color — a thing that sees and chooses, not a system or a creed.
- The full shape of his experiment: decades of provocations staged to see whether anything makes the machine react. This is the Control Room confession.
- That Suhail cannot be killed by ordinary weapons, the shimmering blade being the sole exception. **Discovered at the finale:** a normal-weapon attempt to stop him fails; only the blade he gave the PC in 3b works. Pays off the centuries-old reveal (a 200-year-old man does not die to a guild sword) and re-loads the handoff.

### 1. The Gate

The approach has been days through the deepest scrub. The ground here is tainted with the Color — that purple-beyond-purple the eye refuses to file — and the sky above is a riot of pale hues refracted into strange threads, the whole vault of it hard to hold a gaze on. The PC has taken to squinting, to closing their eyes a moment to rest them, and the after-image persists anyway, burned in behind the lids. Somewhere in the last day a wrongness has settled into the body: tired, a low headache, a sense of being faintly unwell that may be the phosphorescence of everything and may be something else.

Then the factory. A vast concrete bulk risen out of the scrub — windowless, brutalist, smokestacks pouring black into the refracted sky. What it makes is not apparent. Nor where raw material comes in, nor where finished goods go out, nor who hauls them. No yards, no roads worn by traffic, no loading. It is as though someone built a factory for the idea of a factory and left it running. It reads, against all of the above, as the one solid thing for miles — a place that might be safe to rest in, to recover a little. It is also, plainly, intriguing.

The PC approaches the manufactory gate and is refused — the guild signet, whatever it counts for elsewhere, counts for nothing here. Then a wild-haired man in a vagrant's rags walks up out of the scrub and through the gate without slowing. The guards begin a motion to stop him and it dies before it commits — they know him, or know what he is, and the knowing pulls the hand back down. He is past them before they have finished deciding not to act.

Then he turns and calls the PC through on his own authority. The PC enters under his name: a vagrant's name, accepted at a gate where a guild member's standing failed a moment before.

He gives nothing away about why. Asked, he deflects — lightly, almost playfully, already a step down the corridor. *Plenty of time. Long way down.* The unanswered question of how a man dressed like this walks where a guild officer cannot is the first thing the PC carries inside.

### 2. The Lamp

Just inside, in the first dark, Suhail searches a supply closet and hands the PC a carbide lamp. *You'll want this. I won't.* He offers his name then — Suhail — as though it had only just occurred to him they hadn't been introduced.

The PC has the presence of mind to ask whether he is someone important. He allows that he is, or was. He used to be brothers with most of the men who run the Kesharat now — rode with them, got into the kind of trouble young men get into, back in the good years when they were all young together. *A long time ago. They chose a different path.* He still likes trouble. He just does it alone now.

He is filthy, sun-baked, dust worked into every seam of him — and not a day past his mid-thirties under all of it. The arithmetic of "a long time ago" doesn't sit right against the face saying it, and he offers nothing to fix the discrepancy.

Then he turns, and in the lightless room the Color is the only thing the PC can see of him: his eyes, intense with it, the same purple-beyond-purple from the scrub now banked and burning behind a living face. Out in all that glare it never registered. Here it is unmistakable. He reads the look before it becomes a question and names it himself — Kesharat privilege. *Reserved for those the Lattice takes a particular interest in.* He moves through the dark at ease. The PC has a circle six feet wide.

### 3. The Floor — three spaces, any order

The Lamp leaves the PC standing on the open floor of the place, and the floor has no walls the light can find. What it has is sound, and the sound comes from three directions. One way, the shuffle and click of the assembly benches. Another, the deep slow slam of the presses, felt through the boots before it is heard. A third, a shriek and grind of metal on metal loud enough to flatten everything near it. Suhail lets the PC choose. You go toward a sound; when you are done with that space, the other two are still calling, and you go toward one of them. There is no order to it. Only when all three have been seen does Suhail turn the PC deeper, toward the door at the back and the room behind it.

**Structure (for decompose).** 3a / 3b / 3c form a fully-connected triangle: from any space the PC can head toward either of the other two by following its sound. Each space sets a visited tag; intra-floor moves hide once their target is visited (1a.ii). The route to the Control Room (Scene 4) is gated on all three visited tags. The husk-regard beats in **3a** and **3c** each offer the intervention that marks `foundry.challenge` (0–2 total). **3b** has no husk beat — it is the blade-and-history space, and the one place the PC acquires `shimmering_blade`, which the finale requires. Suhail accompanies the PC throughout; his husks-are-empty thesis is made *co-equally* in each space, never escalated across them.

#### 3a. Assembly — *She can't hear you.*

The lamp opens a circle of light into a dark that has no edges. Inside the circle: a handful of workers at a bench, hands moving on the same small clockwork assembly, unhurried and exact. Beyond the circle, nothing the eye can use — but the sound. Shuffle and click in every direction, layered and overlapping, more hands than the dark will give up. There is no telling how many. The room takes the lamp and gives back six feet of it.

They wear the same drab uniform, the same cropped haircut — men, women, no difference made, every age from the very young to the very old. And when one tilts toward the light it has Suhail's eyes: the Color banked and burning, the same purple-beyond-purple, in face after face after face.

The PC tries to engage one — a word, a hand on the shoulder, anything. Nothing answers. Then out of the black Suhail's voice, delighted: *"SHE CAN'T HEAR YOU."* The words go out and keep going, echo stacking on echo, and only then does the size of the place land — vast, vaulted, full of unseen work.

Then the cruelties, and they are aimed: whichever worker the PC reaches for is the one Suhail picks. He knocks its piece to the floor, tips its bin of pins across the ground. The husk stumbles after the scattered work, mechanical and untroubled, and gathers it back. *Nothing minds. They only tidy.* The PC can set the work right, sort the pins, steady a hand — at no real cost. A mark on `foundry.challenge`.

The theory comes with the act — that they are hollow, that whatever lived in them is gone, that this is tidying and not suffering. He keeps proving it, lightly, and watches the PC's face for the objection. He makes the same case in each of these spaces, never louder, never further; he needs the PC to sit with it, to find it at least arguable.

#### 3b. The Cutting Machine — *the offered weapon*

Toward the shriek, and it grows until it is the only thing in the world: one machine, taller than a house, working in the dark. It is cutting metal — a blade driving down through stacked plate, lathing each one into a shape for some later assembly. Where the edge bites, the metal flares with the Color and then dulls, the way a worked piece holds heat after the tool has passed, cooling from bright to a low glow. The noise is the worst of it: metal on metal, a shriek and grind that fills the whole space. Suhail is talking. The PC can see his mouth moving and cannot make out a word.

He walks out of the lamplight, toward the machine. A moment later it thunks, drops in pitch, and grinds down to silence. It takes the PC a moment to find him again — by his voice, off in the dark to one side, already mid-sentence.

He is working on the machine now. He throws the isolators, waits out the cycle, keys open a housing along the cutting head, and draws the blade out of it: the length of an arm, the edge still faintly lit. He wraps the tang in a rag. His hands know the order of the work. Where a man dressed like he sleeps in ditches learned the shutdown for a machine like this, he does not say.

He keeps talking. He was there, he says, when this all started. Nearly 200 years ago. His clan had heard rumors of an order of monks who guarded a treasure. Some warriors said it was bad luck to steal from them, and Suhail wishes they had listened. The monks put up a fight, Suhail pantomimes with the blade as he narrates, cutting ragged, glowing slices through crates of materials. He comes to the climax of the story; Suhail against the monastery head master.

Then the blade is at the PC's throat. *I could open you here. No one would mark it.* He is looking past the PC, at a monk centuries dead, and for a moment does not seem to know which of them is in front of him. Then it passes. He lowers the blade, makes a short tired sound that is not a laugh, and presses it into the PC's hands. *Take it. You'll rest easier with me unarmed.*

If the PC asks, Suhail explains what they found. An ornate seal, tall as a man, etched into the living stone. the seal was inlaid with gold and silver, hammered into the carving. some sort of demon or guardian deity. not the treasure they hoped for, but the warriors made the best of it and started to pry loose the metal. then the blinding flash, the first time they had seen the Color. the sickness, the afterglow. then it passed. 

At first no one marked it. But those who had been there developed a mania over the coming weeks. a desperate need to organize and tidy and bring structure to their surroundings. Isolated at first; fitful re-ordering of objects. optimization of time spent during each day. but eventually it would take hold of the clan. war plans became complex, layered, meticulous. conquered villages were absorbed into a bureaucratic structure of administration, not left to burn. supply routes ensured the army always had fresh provisions and could focus on larger strategic objectives, not filling their bellies today.

As the clan met with success, the "ordering sickness" term gave way to "the lattice" as an ambient, ordering structure that aligned the growing empire.

#### 3c. The Press — *You're grieving a machine.*

The slam is felt before it is heard, deep and slow as a heartbeat, up through the boots and into the teeth. The lamplight finds the nearest press: a black mass coming down on its bed and lifting, coming down and lifting, a worker feeding it — sliding a blank under the die, drawing the hand back, sliding the next. The timing is exact and it is close.

Suhail forces nothing here. He stoops, picks a tool off the floor — a heavy die-key, a spanner, something with weight — and lobs it underhand into the press's bed, into the strike zone, the way a man tosses a stick for a dog. Then he steps back to watch.

A husk goes after it. Not the one feeding the press — another, out of the dark, drawn to the dropped thing the way the benches were drawn to scattered pins. It gets down and starts in under the die, reaching, untroubled, while the ram comes down and lifts and comes down. It does not look at the press. It does not hurry. It will go in after the tool whether the stroke is up or down.

The PC can stop it — haul it back by the collar, kick the tool clear, foul the feed — close enough to the stroke to feel the air move ahead of it. The risk is the PC's own. *A mark on `foundry.challenge`.*

Suhail watches the PC, not the husk. *Residue,* he says. *A reflex in old meat. You're grieving a machine.* And the more the thing looks like a person — a slack face, a hand reaching with something that is almost want — the more he says it, in the voice of a man who needs it to land more than he needs it to be true.

### 4. Control Room — *All of it comes down.* / the finale

The control room is a wall of panels, most of them dark, a few lit with the Color. Suhail knows which ones. He opens a cabinet and works through it without hurry — the same practiced hands that opened the cutting head — throwing governors, hauling levers past their stops, taking the brakes off the place one system at a time: the furnaces that feed the stacks, the presses, every safeguard built to hold the plant short of tearing itself apart.

One control he leaves. He rests a hand on it and tells the PC plainly what it does. Thrown, the plant runs away with itself — heat and pressure climbing with nothing left to bleed them off. It is not quick. It builds. There is the time it takes to walk out at a steady pace and no more than that; go now and they go safe. The place comes down after, on its own, with no one left in it but the husks. *A small thing,* he says. *Noise and broken glass. They are already gone.*

Then the truth, his hand still resting on the control. Decades of provocations, each one watched for a response that never came. Burned depots that were rebuilt better. Killed administrators who were replaced faster. Disruptions the machine absorbed and, somehow, used. He came to see whether this one is any different — whether there is a scale of damage the entity cannot simply route around. He does not think there is. He wants to be wrong.

The pause. Then the question he cannot answer: whether even this experiment was designed for him. Whether the urge to poke at the machine, to find its limits, to make it react — whether that too was placed in him. Every past failure is one more proof he cannot refute.

Then the **counter check.**

**Low challenge** — he presents the act as a switch to be thrown. *Press it, or stop me. Now.*

To stop him is to kill him, and that is not the simple thing it looks. A man the deal made deathless does not fall to ordinary steel. If the PC draws their own weapon on him, the blow lands and does nothing — he looks at them, almost sorry, his hand never leaving the control. The shimmering blade is the exception, the one edge that bites him as it bit the seal; the PC has carried it since the cutting floor, and only now does *why he gave it over* arrive, with the weight of it in their hand. (Decompose: the kill outcome is gated `has shimmering_blade`; an ordinary-weapon attempt is a separate, failing outcome.)

If the PC stops him with the blade: it does the work cleanly — a thing built to part steel plate does not strain at a man. The plant is already running away; his death does not call it back. They walk out ahead of the smoke.

If the PC lets him finish: on the road away from the smoke, a group of engineers is heading toward the site. They heard the blast. They ask, with professional interest, whether the structure came down cleanly.

**High challenge** — he does not offer a switch. He offers a question, asked over the emptiest husk in the room: *Why do you care about them? They lost themselves long ago. To end one is to break a machine. What are they to you?* The answer is not winning the argument over whether the husks are real — it is refusing the terms. Not *I will prove they are real,* but *I will not make my conduct wait on a verdict the Lattice made unknowable.* This is also a refusal of the specific verdict Suhail has spent the descent constructing. The PC is declining to be the instrument he has spent the whole descent building.

Suhail gives the PC the move in plain words — *you will lose your mind trying to outthink the machine; do what you believe is right, and answer to yourself* — because he has known it all along and has been too alone to make it. That is why he needed someone to follow him into the dark. The PC makes it, and they walk out together. The prismatic sword is in the PC's hands the whole way. The road is quiet.

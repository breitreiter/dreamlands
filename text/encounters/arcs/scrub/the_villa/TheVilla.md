# The Villa (Scrub Tier 1)

A guild merchant is dead in a mesa villa two hours from the rail line.
A page intercepts the PC on the road: the merchant's barrister is outside
arguing jurisdiction with a pair of Kesharat officials. Someone the barrister
trusts — someone not already associated with the guild — needs to go in
through the side gate, find the ledgers, and make sure nothing else
survives the night.

**Deeper concept:** The Lattice propagates through commerce. The
geometrically perfect crystals the merchant was trading are not products
— they are nodes. Every collector who installs one, every architect who
incorporates one into a building, extends the mesh one connection further.
The merchant did not discover a trade route. The merchant *was* the trade
route. The horror is that the crystals do not need to do anything visible
to the people who handle them. They simply need to travel.

**Hook:** The PC is flagged down by a page on the mesa road. Brief,
urgent. The barrister is occupied at the gate, the Kesharat officials are
not going away, and the clock is running.

**Clutch:** The PC finds the ledgers and, tucked behind them, a journal.
Reading it takes time they do not have. When they come up from the cave
and face the barrister, they have to decide what they know, what the
barrister should know, and what should cease to exist.

# Scene Sketch

## Opening

The page is young, plainly dressed, doing a poor job of looking casual
at the side of the road. When the PC comes within earshot, they close the
distance quickly and speak without preamble: a guild merchant named Ferath
Solan is dead in his villa on the mesa. The barrister, one Ceel Vastand,
has been outside since this morning arguing with two Kesharat officials
about which authority has jurisdiction over the estate and its contents.
Nobody has been inside yet.

Vastand needs someone not formally attached to the guild to enter through
the servants' gate at the back, locate the merchant's ledgers, and remove
them before the Kesharat get access. If there is anything else inside that
looks compromising, Vastand wants it removed or destroyed at the PC's
discretion. The page does not know what "anything else" means. Neither,
apparently, does Vastand — but Solan was a cautious man and kept careful
records, and there are apparently customers in those records who would
prefer not to be found there.

The PC is not a guild officer. They have no legal standing here. They are
also the only person available on short notice who Vastand believes will
not steal anything.

The side gate is unlocked.

## The Villa

The house has the quality of a place that was lived in carefully and then
not lived in at all. Shuttered windows. Weeks of dust on the kitchen
table. Dried herbs still hanging but long past use. A bed slept in on
only one side, the other piled with — something, covered by a sheet.
Solan appears to have reduced his footprint room by room until he occupied
only the study and whatever is below it.

The study door at the end of the hall is locked with a good lock. There
is a matching key on a cord around Solan's neck. Solan is in the back
corner of the kitchen, seated, as though he sat down deliberately and did
not get up. He has been dead for roughly two days. The expression on his
face is not one the PC will find easy to characterize.

The cord is intact. The key slides free without resistance. He is not
holding the crystals as tightly as the journal suggests he planned to.

## The Study

The ledgers are on the desk — five of them, covering several years of
trade. They are well-kept. Routine at first glance: commodity routes,
margin notes, supplier contacts in coded shorthand. Less routine on close
inspection: a column of receipts from a market in Aldgate for a line item
described only as "formation goods," starting small and growing, by the
end of the fourth ledger, to a figure that explains the villa and the cave
and the lock on the door.

The fifth ledger is not a ledger. It is a journal, tucked inside the
cover of what appears to be a ledger, in a different hand than the
records — looser at first, tighter later, and in the final pages tight in
a way that stops looking like handwriting.

There are twelve legible entries in non-sequential order; pages have come
loose, some are stuck together, some are water-damaged at the edges. The
PC can piece together the arc: a windfall, a trade, an obsession, a
villa, a cave, a paranoia that stopped being paranoia and became something
worse, and an ending that does not explain how Solan died because the
final entry is not text.

The final page is covered edge to edge in geometric diagrams — dense,
precise, executed with a fine-nibbed pen in what appears to be multiple
overlapping sessions. At first glance they resemble architectural
cross-sections. On closer inspection they are attempting something that
two dimensions cannot contain: geometric solids rendered from angles that
should not be possible, each face shaded to suggest depth in contradictory
directions, lines that appear to intersect and do not, figures that seem
to be the same shape approached from several positions simultaneously. The
work is patient and deliberate. In the bottom corner, very small: *Not
finished. Not close.*

## The Cave

The study has a second door, low, set into the back wall. Stone steps
leading down.

The cave is real. The walls glitter in torchlight — geode formations in
clusters, some the size of a fist, some larger, a few as tall as the PC's
waist. The geometry of them is visible even in poor light: they are not
merely crystalline, they are *structured*, faceted in patterns that do not
occur in undisturbed rock. Some have been cut loose from the walls and
stacked near the steps for transport. Most have not.

The light from the formations is very faint — barely perceptible without
total darkness — but it is there. It is not a color the PC has a name for.
It illuminates the nearby stone in a way that makes the surrounding rock
look like an imitation of stone. Looking at it directly produces nothing
useful. Looking at it from the corner of the eye produces something the PC
would rather not examine.

The PC can take crystals from the cave. There is no obvious consequence
for touching them briefly. Whether brief is the operative word is a
question the PC will not be able to answer inside the arc.

## The Gate

Vastand's argument outside has not resolved. The Kesharat officials have
sent for a senior functionary and settled in to wait. Vastand has perhaps
an hour, possibly less, before the question of jurisdiction gets decided
over his head.

The page is waiting near the side gate. Vastand wants a report.

## The Choice

### Destroy the journal, cover the ledgers, report success

The PC burns the journal in the study brazier and brings out the ledgers
with the "formation goods" column on clean pages — or destroys those pages
too, if they're being thorough. Vastand gets a set of books that will
satisfy the guild and irritate the Kesharat officials by containing
nothing they can use.

The PC reports there was nothing unusual inside. A tidy merchant's estate.

A Cunning check. The Kesharat officials have been outside a locked house
for a day and a half; they know something was worth locking. The
functionary they sent for has not arrived yet but will. If the check
succeeds, the PC and Vastand are gone before jurisdiction is resolved and
the question dies on the mesa with the ash in the brazier. If the check
fails, a functionary notes a specific discrepancy — the weight of the
ledgers, the smell of burning paper, the unlocked study — and the guild
and Vastand face an inquiry. The PC does not face one, having no formal
standing, but the barrister will remember.

### Destroy the journal, tell Vastand the truth

The PC tells Vastand what the journal described: crystal trade, the cave,
the extent of what "formation goods" actually represents. Vastand listens
with the expression of a man calculating. He is not surprised that Solan
was doing something he shouldn't. He is not certain he wants to know how
far it went.

Whether this benefits or costs the PC depends on Vastand's read of what
the information is worth and to whom. A barrister who can suppress the
cave's existence has something to sell. A barrister who reports it to the
guild has discharged his duty. Vastand is practical before he is moral.
The PC has told him the truth; what Vastand does with it is his own
problem.

The Kesharat officials eventually get into the cave. The formation goods
column in the surviving ledgers becomes a matter of administrative record.
Several of Solan's customers have a difficult season.

### Give the journal to Vastand

The PC hands over the journal intact and lets Vastand read it.

Vastand is a careful reader. He takes the entries in the order they are
presented and does not skip ahead. He reaches the final page and sits with
it for a long moment. Then he closes the journal and asks the PC how much
of this they believe.

The conversation that follows is the most dangerous thing in the arc. Not
because Vastand is hostile — he is not. Because he is intelligent, and
the journal makes a kind of sense to an intelligent man, and the PC has
just given him the first twelve steps down a road that the journal itself
proves leads somewhere the merchant could not find words for. Vastand will
not become Solan. He is a barrister and a practical man. But he will take
the journal with him, and he will keep turning it over, and the part of it
that makes sense will keep making sense, and the part of it that does not
make sense will keep appearing in the corner of his vision long after the
rest of it fades.

The PC walks away knowing they handed something to someone who was not
immune.

### Keep the journal

The PC takes the journal. Not for evidence. For the same reason Solan
kept the crystals — something about it does not permit leaving it behind.

Vastand gets the ledgers and a clean report. The Kesharat officials
eventually gain access to an estate with a cave in it and some empty
brackets on the walls where crystals used to be. The "formation goods"
column is a customs matter. Nobody finds the journal.

On the road some weeks later, the PC wakes before dawn to find their pack
reorganized. Not ransacked — organized, with a particular precision they
do not remember applying. The contents are in good order. Nothing is
missing. The geometry of how the items are arranged is, on reflection,
exactly correct.

They do not mention this to anyone.

# Cast — The Villa

First draft. Coverage over polish. `[?]` marks an open question;
`GAP:` marks something the brief leaves to the writer.

The defining fact of this cast: the central character is dead before the
arc begins and the most dangerous one is a book. Ferath Solan is met only
as a seated corpse and as the voice of his own journal; the servant girl
is met only as a body. The living characters the PC actually speaks with
(the page, Vastand) bracket the arc at its start and end. The horror is
carried by the dead and by the journal's failing hand, not by anyone
present to argue with.

## Ferath Solan — the dead merchant (corpse + journal voice)

The arc's protagonist-by-proxy. Present two ways: a body in the kitchen,
and the writer of the journal the PC reads through the middle of the arc.
Everything the PC learns about him, they learn from his own record and his
corpse. He never speaks aloud and is never alive on screen.

- **Role:** a guild merchant. Cautious, methodical, kept careful coded
  books. Took a single geometrically-perfect crystal as payment on a bad
  debt, discovered (by accident, via a buyer in Aldgate) what such a thing
  was worth, and built the rest of his life around the cave of them he
  bought with the proceeds.
- **The corpse:** seated in the back corner of the kitchen, as though he
  sat down deliberately and did not get up. Dead about two days. The key
  to the study on a cord at his neck, intact. His face is hard to read and
  the PC will not find an easy word for the expression. He is not holding
  the crystals; whatever killed him, it was not a struggle.
  - GAP: cause of death is deliberately unstated. The journal does not
    explain it (the final entry is not text). Do not invent a cause; the
    not-knowing is the point. He simply stopped, below, where the cave gave
    him what he needed and he was not hungry anymore.
- **The journal voice (the load-bearing one):** twelve legible entries,
  non-sequential, some pages loose, stuck, or water-damaged. The hand
  changes across the arc and this is the through-line:
  - **Early:** dry, dispassionate research notes. A merchant pricing an
    unknown. Annoyed he was paid in goods instead of cash. Then the good
    days: short supply, offers he could refuse, sudden fascinating dinner
    invitations.
  - **Middle:** the writing loosens and warms with acquisition (the cave,
    all his), then tightens as he stops selling and starts sketching;
    contracts lapse; the paranoia arrives with cause and then past cause.
    The murder entry is short and flat, the way a man notes where he left
    a tool: "She was bleeding me to take them. It is done. She is in the
    kitchen cabinet."
  - **Late:** the hand shrinks in places to something too fine to read.
    "I stay below now. The cave gives me what I need. I am not hungry
    anymore." Then it stops being text at all and becomes geometric
    diagrams worked over many sittings, with one note in the corner: "Not
    finished. Not close."
- **Knows:** the trade cold (routes, margins, coded shorthand). What a cave
  of these is worth. His own descent, recorded but never understood as what
  it is.
- **Cannot:** name what the crystals are or what they did to him. See that
  his sickness was the crystals and not the girl. The paranoia (followed,
  poisoned) reads to him as ordinary danger; the reader can see it is the
  Color working in him, but Solan never can.
- **Wants:** early, money. Then to control the market. Then, fatally, not
  to part with a single one. By the end, only to stay below.
- **Voice:** a careful merchant's prose, dry and precise, degrading into
  obsession and then into geometry. Never lyrical, even at the end; the
  horror is that he stays factual and clipped while what he is recording
  stops making sense.

## The page — the messenger (Scene 1 only)

- **Role:** a guild errand-runner sent down to the mesa road to catch
  someone Vastand can use. Young, plainly dressed, doing a poor job of
  looking casual until the PC is close enough for him to drop the act.
- **Knows:** the brief Vastand gave him, no more. Solan is dead; Vastand
  has been arguing jurisdiction with two Kesharat at the gate since
  morning; nobody has been inside; the side gate is open. That the PC, with
  guild signet but no face the gate has seen, is exactly who is needed.
- **Cannot:** say what "anything else" in the house means. He does not
  know, and admits it.
- **Wants:** to hand the errand off and be done with the mesa.
- **Voice:** urgent, without preamble, relieved by the sight of guild
  signet. Defers to the PC's standing. Glances toward the mesa more than
  once.

## Ceel Vastand — the barrister (Scene 3 only)

The arc's only living conversation, and its most dangerous moment. The PC
does not meet him until they come back out to the gate; everything before
that, he is an off-screen pressure (arguing jurisdiction, an hour left on
the clock).

- **Role:** the dead merchant's guild barrister. Trusts the PC because the
  PC is guild enough to handle a dead man's books and unknown enough at
  the gate not to be marked going in.
- **Knows:** the legal situation cold; that Solan was cautious and that his
  records hold customers who would rather not be found. Does not know about
  the cave or the journal until the PC tells or shows him.
- **Cannot:** be immune. This is the load-bearing fact of his character and
  of the "give him the journal" ending: he is intelligent, and the journal
  makes a kind of sense to an intelligent man. He will not become Solan, he
  is too practical, but the part of it that makes sense will keep making
  sense and the part that does not will keep appearing at the corner of his
  vision.
- **Wants:** the ledgers out before the Kesharat get them; the guild
  protected; the situation closed cleanly. He is practical before he is
  moral, and he does not pretend otherwise.
- **Voice:** measured, a careful man's reading. Reads in the order given,
  does not skip ahead. "How much of this do you believe?" is his, and he
  does not wait for the answer. When the PC tells him the cost (the cave,
  the girl), he listens "like a man doing arithmetic" and does not say
  whether knowing is a complaint or a thank-you.

## The servant girl — the body in the cabinet (Scene 2, Middle Pages)

- **Role:** hired by Solan from a nearby village after a bad night, in the
  grip of the paranoia. He decided she was poisoning him to take the
  crystals and killed her. The journal names it flatly; the body confirms
  it. There is nothing on or near her that suggests she poisoned anyone.
  The sickness was his own.
- **The body:** folded into the kitchen cabinet where Solan wrote he put
  her, some weeks dead (longer than Solan himself). Small, and young.
- **Function:** the moral floor of the arc. The discovery is what turns the
  reading from a dead man's strange decline into a murder, and converts the
  crystals from "worth killing for" into "they make a man do this." She
  never speaks and was never seen alive; she is the cost made physical.
- **GAP:** village of origin and name left open. Default: a Tashkari girl
  from a village near the mesa, unnamed. Naming her may sharpen the gut
  punch; leaving her unnamed keeps her the anonymous cost Solan made her.
  Writer's call; lean unnamed unless a downstream beat needs the name.

## The two Kesharat officials — the clock (off-screen)

- **Role:** two Kesharat at the front gate, arguing jurisdiction over the
  estate and its contents with Vastand since morning. They have sent for a
  senior functionary and settled in to wait. Never seen up close; the PC
  goes in the back precisely to avoid them.
- **Function:** the arc's clock and its only mechanical threat. They know
  something was worth locking (a locked house, a day and a half outside
  it). In the burn ending, the functionary they sent for is the thing the
  Cunning check is racing.
- **Voice/manner (canon, `text/lore/scrub.md`):** polite, procedural,
  certain. They do not negotiate in good faith so much as wait for
  procedure to decide over Vastand's head. No hostility, no haste; the
  pressure is bureaucratic inevitability, not menace. Keep them entirely
  off-screen unless a future beat needs them; the brief never brings the PC
  face to face with them and it should stay that way.

## The PC

A Traders Guild member (signet ring, travels alone), guild enough to be
trusted with a dead colleague's books, unknown enough at this gate to pass
unmarked. Renaissance-era eye: no word for the crystals, the cave-light, or
the Color, and no frame for what the journal's final page is attempting.
Reads the trade like a fellow merchant (recognizes the coded shorthand, the
hidden column, the good-problem of short supply), which is exactly what
makes the descent legible and the horror personal.

## NPCs implied but not present (do not stage)

- **The Lattice.** Not a character. The crystals are its nodes; the horror
  premise is that it propagates through commerce, that Solan did not find a
  trade route but *was* one. It never speaks and never acts on screen. The
  cave-light and the reorganized pack (keep ending) are its only direct
  touch.
- **The Aldgate buyers / the customers in the books.** Off-screen. The
  "customers who would prefer not to be found" in the ledgers, and the
  buyer who paid the absurd sum. Named only as a market and a record.
- **The broker / the original seller.** Mentioned in the journal (the tip
  to the villa, the leaned-on first seller). Not seen. Abstractions.
- **The senior functionary.** Sent for, not arrived. The thing the burn
  ending's clock is racing. Stays an abstraction.

# Lens — The Early Pages

How the PC sees this scene. Steering for color: whose eye, what to notice,
in what key. Stance, not facts.

## Perceptual
A merchant reading another merchant's books by lamplight. This is the most
professional, most legible part of the journal, and the PC reads it as a
peer: the dry research notes pricing an unknown, the annoyance at being paid
in goods, the absurd Aldgate sum, the good days of short supply and
refusable offers. Notice what a trader notices: the discipline of the
record, the coded shorthand, the shape of a market opening up. When the
optional spoke goes to the ledgers, read the hidden formation-goods column
with a trader's eye for what a sum like that means. The hand here is even
and dry; notice that it is.

## Social / tonal
Dry, controlled, faintly enviable before it sours. The register is a
merchant's professional interest in a colleague's clever run, and color's
job through most of it is that competence and momentum, not dread. The one
seam, planted late, is the turn: the sellers going quiet, the Kesharat
clamping down, the first conviction he was followed. Render that as a chill
under business, not yet horror, a man's luck closing while he is still
writing like a man who expects it to open again. Do not import the cave or
the later wrongness; this scene only knows the trade.

# Lens — The Mesa Road (Arrival)

How the PC sees this scene. Steering for color: whose eye, what to notice,
in what key. Stance, not facts.

## Perceptual
A guild merchant on the mesa road toward dusk, flagged down by a page who
is bad at looking casual. The first half of the scene is a roadside
errand, read with a trader's eye: the boy's plain dress, his relief at
guild papers, the shape of the favor being asked. The second half is
entering an empty house in failing light, a stranger's eye on another
merchant's home. Read the house as a portrait of its dead owner, three
ages in one frame: the faded splendor of a villa that was elegant once
(fine bones, good materials gone to ruin, beauty in decay), the long
dilapidation he bought into cheap, and on top of it the recent squalor of a
man who shuttered the windows from inside and lived for months in a corner
of a house too big for him. Notice the body the way you would notice it,
plainly, and the key, and the good lock on the one kept room. Name nothing
supernatural; there is nothing supernatural here yet.

## Social / tonal
An opening, and mostly mundane. Color's job is baseline normal tipping
quietly off-true. On the road it is ordinary: a guild errand, slightly
furtive, nothing a merchant has not done before. Inside, the register cools
into unease without ever becoming horror: a house that was kept and then
abandoned by the same careful hands, a dead colleague sat down in a corner
as if on purpose. The wrongness is restraint and absence, not menace.
Nothing moves, nothing threatens; the PC is alone with a dead man's tidy
decline. Record the seams (the narrowed house, the seated corpse, the lock
better than the room needs) and let the journal do the rest.

# Lens — The Middle Pages

How the PC sees this scene. Steering for color: whose eye, what to notice,
in what key. Stance, not facts.

## Perceptual
The same reader, now reading past the point where it is business. The hand
on the page is loosening with acquisition (the cave, all his) and then
tightening as the selling stops and the sketching starts. Notice the
turn from merchant to obsessive in the writing itself: contracts lapsing,
buyers stiffed, a man who cannot part with a single one. Read the paranoia
with the doubled awareness the journal allows, that it began with cause and
went past it. Read the murder entry for exactly what it is: short, flat,
clerical, a man noting where he left a tool. When the optional spoke sends
the PC back to the cabinet, the eye goes plain and unflinching: a small
body, weeks dead, and nothing on her that bears out a word he wrote.

## Social / tonal
Dread tipping into horror, and this is where it lands hardest, harder than
the cave. The register descends across the scene: unease at the obsession,
real disquiet at the paranoia, and then the floor dropping out at the
cabinet. The murder is rendered cold because *he* rendered it cold; the
horror is the flatness, not gore. The cabinet beat is the moral center of
the whole arc, the point where the crystals stop being merely worth killing
for and become a thing that makes a man do this. Render the body with
restraint and pity, small and young and innocent, and let the
weight come from the contrast with the tool-noting voice that put her there.

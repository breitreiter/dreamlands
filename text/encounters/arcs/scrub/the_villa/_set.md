# Set — The Villa

First draft. `[?]` = open question; `GAP:` = brief leaves to writer.

## Location

A run-down Tashkari villa on the mesa, two hours from the rail line. Tier 1
scrub. The owner had needed cash more than the house; Solan bought it for
what was underneath it. It stands alone; there is no settlement at hand,
which is why a man could keep a cave and a body in it for weeks unremarked.

- **Time of day:** late. The job is framed as making sure nothing else
  "survives the night." GAP: exact hour left loose. Default: the long
  light is gone and the work happens toward dusk and into dark, lamp or
  torch in hand inside the shuttered house.
- **The mesa road:** quiet. The PC is intercepted on it by the page, two
  hours out from the rail line. No other traffic in the scene.
- **The gates:** a front gate where Vastand has stood since morning with
  the two Kesharat, and a servants' (side/back) gate at the rear, unlocked.
  The PC goes in the back so the Kesharat at the front do not mark the
  going-in. The page waits near the side gate for a report.

## The house (a portrait of the man, in three ages)

The house is read like a body, and it tells Solan's story before the journal
does. Three ages sit stacked in one frame, and the house mirrors the man
whose journal the PC carries:

1. **The faded splendor (deep past).** This was an elegant, worldly villa
   once, fine in its bones: good materials, fine proportions, the taste of
   money. Render it through the renaissance eye as the wreck of taste, the
   urbex aesthetic, beauty gone to ruin. (Solan's own worldly, prosperous
   years.)
2. **The dilapidation (what he bought).** It was already run-down when Solan
   bought it cheap from an owner who needed cash more than the house. The
   grandeur had been left to decay before he ever arrived. (Solan's
   withdrawal, the trade going quiet.)
3. **The narrowed squalor (recent).** On top of the long ruin, the most
   recent layer: a man who boarded himself in for months, shuttered the
   windows from inside, and reduced his footprint room by room until he
   occupied only the study and the cave below it. (Solan's final months
   below.)

The mirror is steering for the prose stages, not a thing to state outright
to the player; let it be felt by reading the house in order, not announced.

- **Disuse:** shuttered windows (shut from inside). Weeks of dust on
  surfaces. Dried herbs still hanging in the kitchen, long past use. A bed
  slept in on only one side, the other piled with something under a sheet.
  Fine work showing under the grime: a tiled floor, a carved doorframe,
  stained plaster over what was once good. GAP: what is under the sheet is
  left unspecified in the brief; do not resolve it into a second body or a
  treasure. Default: stored crystals or sketched pages, or simply the
  accumulated clutter of a man who stopped using the room. Keep it an
  unexamined wrongness, not a reveal.
- **The kitchen:** Solan's corpse seated in the back corner (see `_cast`).
  Dried herbs overhead. And the cabinet (below).
- **The kitchen cabinet:** where the servant girl's body is folded, some
  weeks dead, exactly where Solan's journal says he put her. The PC only
  goes back to it if the Middle Pages send them (the `villa.danger`
  discovery). Nothing on or near her suggests poison.

## The study (the one kept room)

- **The door:** at the end of the hall, locked with a good lock. The
  matching key is on a cord at Solan's neck and comes free without
  resistance. The quality of the lock is itself information: he locked this
  room against something.
- **Contents:** a desk; five books on it; a cold brazier in the corner; the
  low second door (below). A life narrowed down to this room.
- **The five books:** four are real ledgers, well-kept, routine at a glance
  (commodity routes, margin notes, coded supplier shorthand). The fifth is
  not a ledger: it is the journal, bound inside a ledger's cover, in a
  different hand than the records.
  - **The hidden column (the `villa.economic_value` discovery):** in the
    real ledgers, a column of Aldgate-market receipts for a line item
    called only "formation goods," small at first and growing by the fourth
    book to a figure that explains the villa, the cave, and the lock.
- **The brazier:** stands cold in the corner. It is the instrument of the
  burn ending; otherwise it is just cold iron in a dead man's room.

## The cave (the only direct contact with the Color)

Reached by the low second door set into the back wall of the study, stone
steps leading down. The PC only goes down if the Last Pages send them (the
`villa.saw_cave` discovery), and the visit costs `+damage_spirits 2`.

- **The formations:** geode clusters in the walls, some fist-sized, some as
  tall as the PC's waist, a few cut loose and stacked near the steps for
  transport (Solan's interrupted trade made physical). They are not merely
  crystalline; they are *structured*, faceted in patterns that do not occur
  in undisturbed rock.
- **The light (canon, `text/lore/scrub.md` "the Colors"):** very faint,
  barely perceptible without total darkness, and not a color the PC has a
  name for. It illuminates nearby stone in a way that makes the surrounding
  rock look like an imitation of stone. Looked at straight on it does
  nothing useful; at the edge of the eye it does something the PC would
  rather not have seen. The PC leaves with the after-image still working.
  - This is the same nameless Color the lore attaches to the Lattice;
    render it through the tier-1 register: faint, isolated, first contact,
    no saturation, no context. The PC has never seen anything like it and
    has no frame for it.
- **The crystals are takeable** with no obvious immediate consequence for
  touching them briefly. Whether brief is the operative word is a question
  the arc never answers. (The keep ending implies it was not brief enough,
  obliquely.)

## What the PC carries in

Whatever they arrived with. A light source matters inside the shuttered
house and the cave (lamp or torch). GAP: do not gate the arc on the PC
owning a torch; assume one is available or the house has one. The brief
does not make light a mechanic and neither should we.

## Time / clock

- **Solan:** dead about two days.
- **The servant girl:** some weeks dead (longer than Solan).
- **Vastand's clock:** perhaps an hour, possibly less, before the question
  of jurisdiction is decided over his head by the senior functionary the
  Kesharat have sent for. This is the only real time pressure; the reading
  of the journal "takes time the PC does not have," which is why the
  Decision is forced rather than exhaustive.

## What the set does NOT contain

Calling out absences so downstream does not invent them:

- **No monster, no fight, no combat.** This arc has no `.fight`, no enemy,
  no creature in the cave or the house. The only mechanical check is the
  Cunning roll in the burn ending. Do not stage a guardian, a thing in the
  dark, or an ambush. The horror is dread and aftermath, not threat.
- **No living person inside the house.** Solan and the girl are both dead.
  The PC is alone in there. Nobody startles them; nothing moves.
- **No active crystal effect.** The crystals do not glow brightly, pulse,
  hum, sing, move, or visibly do anything. The light is faint and the wrong
  color; that is all. Nothing reaches out. Resist making the cave a set
  piece; its power is that it looks almost like an ordinary cave.
- **No Kesharat inside or at the back.** The officials are at the front
  gate, off-screen, the whole time. The PC never meets them.
- **No clear cause of Solan's death on the body.** No wound, no spilled
  cup, no obvious agent. Do not invent one. (See `_cast`.)
- **No second pylon, no rail crew, no signal tower.** This is a private
  estate, not an infrastructure site; keep `signal_array`'s furniture out
  of it. The only shared element is the crystals and the Color.
- **No haggle, no shop, no other survivors of Solan's trade present.** The
  customers are a column in a book, not people in the room.

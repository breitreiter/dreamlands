You pause in a branching wet tunnel to take bearings, bringing the lantern close to the carved wall. The stone is damp under your fingertips, the marks ragged and soft where moisture has worked them. The tunnel bends, splits, and rejoins with no surface logic to it, its scored surface receding into shadow beyond the lantern's reach.

The wall is covered in inscriptions: love oaths, prayers, names, regrets, tally marks. The script shifts every few paces, fine strokes giving way to gouges, then jagged scrawls. Some passages end mid-word, the final letter trailing off. Newer words are carved directly atop older ones, layer on layer, the older letters still visible beneath. The damp seeps into your fingertips where you press the stone, the marks ragged under your touch, edges lifted and soft where moisture has worked them.

A single name appears inscribed a hundred times over, the stone worn pale around the repeated cuts. Messages to family follow, regrets for things unsaid and misdeeds. Tally marks fill columns in five-bar gates by the hundreds, scored hard into the stone. The marks are ragged under your fingers, edges lifted and soft where moisture has worked them.

You bring the lantern closer. Two oaths stand out beneath the shifting script. One reads: "Alain, you swore by the harvest moon. I kneel where the stone drinks tears and lay your name before the deep. If the spirits hear, let them turn you. If they do not, let them judge you as they judge me." The other: "Matthis, I have brought your token and my last candle. The flame gutters, yet I keep vigil. Let the dark teach you constancy. Let it scour from you the forgetfulness that has made this cavern my only witness." Both end with the finality of someone who has run out of options.

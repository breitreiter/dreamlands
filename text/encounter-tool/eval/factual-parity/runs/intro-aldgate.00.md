You arrive at Aldgate at the end of a weeks-long caravan crossing. The road shows deep ruts worn hard by wheels, and the dry stink of pack beasts and old leather hangs in the air after weeks in close company. The caravan is halted outside the gate, surrounded by weathered crates stacked unevenly, trade cloth wrapped and roped, and dusty barrels in a row. Mules shift in their traces, heads dropping and lifting, not moving on. Imperial banners hang above the gate.

The caravan master stands with imperial functionaries clustering around him, their voices rising. He turns toward you, posture stiff, and gives a quiet look that asks for help. The mules shift in their traces, heads dropping and lifting, while the road stays quiet around the stopped caravan.

You don't step in. The caravan master can sort his own affairs. The caravan idles in the road, the ruts worn deep with wheel-tracks, dried hard. Nothing moves but the wind and the mules, their heads dropping and lifting, not moving on. The dry stink of pack beasts and old leather hangs in the air after weeks in close company.

You notice an unfamiliar weight on your hand. The caravan idles in the road, the wind stirring the imperial banners above the gate, but nothing moves. The dry stink of pack beasts and old leather hangs in the air, and the dust settles around the stopped wagons.

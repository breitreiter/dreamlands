You enter the central plaza of the domed city. Torben stands at the edge, sketching in his notebook, muttering to himself. The plaza stretches out as a hub, with five major corridors branching off, each leading deeper into the city, plus service passages between them. A dome overhead casts a sourceless, directionless light that is not torch-fire and not sun. The walls and floor are made of a single seamless material, with no seams or joins visible anywhere you look.

At the plaza entrance stands a geometric statue on a plinth, angular, non-representational, no human or animal forms. Torben identifies it as Revathi devotional geometry: planes and angles, placed to observe whoever enters. The statue reads as a sentinel or overseer, not decoration.

Down one corridor, the commons opens: a long room with two rows of tables fixed to the floor on metal stems, benches either side. Dust undisturbed across every surface. At the far end, a counter and a preparation area with flat work surfaces, recessed basins with drainage channels cut into the floor, and dark wall-panels behind. Torben carries a lantern; the dome-light does not reach here. The circle of pale surface where his lantern moved the dust reveals the material underneath is featureless. Everything is where you would expect a kitchen to put it. Nothing is a size or shape you would expect.

Another corridor opens into a high-ceilinged hall. A long counter splits the space: one side is an open floor, the other has work-stations built into the wall, each with a recessed shelf and writing surface. Behind them, heavy industrial shelves line the wall, and at the far end, a sealed wide door marks a loading bay.

The steps are shallow and wide, built for a stride longer than yours. Torben's lantern barely reaches the curved walls, leaving the steps above in shadow. The air dries and thins as you climb, a faint current from somewhere above stirring the dust. From the top platform, the city is far larger than it appeared, radial streets extend to the dome wall in every direction. The dome glass curves close overhead, near enough to hit with a stone.

Torben taps his pencil against his notebook. "Assuming the light fades," he mutters, "assuming it isn't something else." He looks up at the dome, his lantern held low. "Can't account for it." The light above remains steady and even, coming from nowhere you can see. You stand on the platform, the city spread below in radial streets extending to the dome wall in every direction. The dome glass curves close overhead, near enough to hit with a stone. Torben walks ahead, notebook in hand, not talking.

You enter the central plaza of an alien underground city. The dome overhead glows with a sourceless, directionless light, not torch-fire and not sun. The walls and floor are a single seamless material, with no seam or join anywhere you look. Five major arteries extend from the plaza, along with service passages, all leading deeper into the city.

At the plaza entrance stands a geometric statue on a plinth, angular and non-representational with no human or animal forms. Torben sketches in his notebook, muttering. "Revathi devotional geometry," he says, tapping the plinth with his knuckle. It makes a flat, dead sound. "Planes and angles. It's meant to observe whoever enters. A sentinel, not decoration."

Down one corridor, Torben's lantern reveals a commons: a long room with two rows of tables fixed to the floor on metal stems, benches either side. Dust lies in an even film across every surface. At the far end is a counter and a preparation area with flat work surfaces, recessed basins with drainage channels, and dark wall-panels. Everything is where you would expect a kitchen to put it, but nothing is a size or shape you would expect. The circle of pale floor where Torben's lantern has moved the dust is featureless underneath.

Another corridor opens into the exchange: a high-ceilinged hall split by a long counter. One side is an open floor, the other has work-stations built into the wall, each with a recessed shelf and writing surface. Behind them, heavy industrial shelving lines the wall, and a loading bay with a sealed wide door sits at the far end.

The dome-light thins to nothing a short way down the corridor. Torben strikes his lantern without comment, as if he'd counted the steps. You follow him to the watchtower stair. Each step is shallow and wide, built for a stride longer than a human's. The curved walls are barely lit by his lantern. The air grows dry and thin as you climb, with a faint current from somewhere above.

From the top platform, the city is far larger than it appeared. Radial streets extend to the dome wall in every direction, and the glass dome curves close overhead, near enough to hit with a stone. Torben walks ahead in the archive corridor, lantern raised, notebook in hand, not talking. He looks up at the sourceless light. "Assuming the light fades," he mutters, "assuming it isn't something else."

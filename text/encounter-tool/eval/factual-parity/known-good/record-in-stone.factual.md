You pause in a branching wet tunnel, bringing the lantern close to the carved wall to take bearings. The tunnel bends, splits, and rejoins with no surface logic to it. The wall is covered in inscriptions: love oaths, prayers, names, regrets, and tally marks. The script shifts every few paces, fine calligraphy giving way to jagged scrawls, then deep gouges. Some passages end mid-word, the final letter trailing off. Newer words are carved directly atop older ones, layer on layer, the older letters still legible beneath. The damp seeps into your fingertips where you press the stone, the marks ragged under your touch, edges lifted and soft where moisture has worked them.

A single name appears inscribed a hundred times over, the stone worn pale around it. You see messages to family, regrets for things unsaid, and confessions of misdeeds. Tally marks fill columns in the stone, five-bar gates by the hundreds, scored hard into the surface. Two quoted oath-fragments are legible. One reads: "Alain, you swore by the harvest moon. I kneel where the stone drinks tears and lay your name before the deep. If the spirits hear, let them turn you. If they do not, let them judge you as they judge me." Another says: "Matthis, I have brought your token and my last candle. The flame gutters, yet I keep vigil. Let the dark teach you constancy. Let it scour from you the forgetfulness that has made this cavern my only witness." Both end in the register of someone who has run out of recourse.

The lantern light catches the uneven face of the wall, shadow pooling in the deeper cuts. Behind you, the tunnel's scored surface recedes into shadow beyond the lantern's reach.

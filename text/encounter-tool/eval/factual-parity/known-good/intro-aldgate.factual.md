You arrive at Aldgate after weeks on the road. The ruts in the road are worn deep with wheel-tracks, dried hard, and the dry stink of pack beasts and old leather hangs in the air after so many nights in close company. The caravan is halted outside the gate, surrounded by weathered crates stacked unevenly, trade cloth wrapped and roped, and dusty barrels in a row. Mules stand in their traces, heads dropping and lifting, not moving on. Imperial banners hang above the gate.

The caravan master is caught in a growing dispute with imperial functionaries clustering around him. His posture shifts toward you, a quiet look that asks for help. You don't step in; the man can sort his own affairs. The caravan idles in the road. Nothing moves but the wind and the mules. The road gone quiet around the stopped caravan, dust settling after weeks of motion. You notice an unfamiliar weight on your hand.

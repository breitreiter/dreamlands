You are stopped by a girl, no older than thirteen or fourteen, who stands in the middle of the road with her feet planted wide in the dust and her arms folded across her chest. Behind her, the wheat field stands tall and golden, stretching to the horizon. The late-afternoon breeze moves through it in slow, rolling waves, and the air is thick with the smell of dry grain and sun-baked earth. The sun is already slanting low, casting an amber light across the field.

At the far edge, near a listing wooden fence, her grandfather works alone with a hand sickle. He is bent double, moving slowly, and pauses between cuts to straighten his back with a hitch, one hand pressed to his spine.

"You're not passing," the girl says, her voice firm. "Not unless you help us."

She steps closer, her sunburnt nose and the smudge of dirt on her cheek plain to see. Her braid is coming loose. "Three hands we hired," she says, her voice cracking just once before she swallows. "They left for Aldgate. The docks pay better. And the rains come tomorrow. The wheat will rot. We don't eat this winter."

Her desperation shows through the bravado. She balls her fists at her sides. "Please," she says. "We have nothing else to trade." She holds out a small bundle of fresh bread and cheese. "This is all. My grandmother made it."

Her eyes flick to the signet ring on your hand, then back up to your face. "You're with the guild," she says. "You have coin. Or time. Something to spare."

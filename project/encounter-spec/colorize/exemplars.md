# Color Exemplars — the gold standard

Hand-authored color the author actually wanted, for the **Signal Array**
opening (`dungeons/signal_array`). Reconstructed from memory after the
originals were lost. **Do not lose these again — they are the taste anchor
for the whole project.** The spec in `NOTES.md` is derived from these, not
the other way around.

> Note: this repo is not under version control. If that changes, these are
> the first thing to commit.

---

## The exemplars

1. the sound of the wind whistling through the metal structure
2. the strange metal construction of the tower itself — the PC is from a
   renaissance-era society, so a radio tower is nearly magical
3. the oppressive heat and sun
4. the strange way the workers had laid out their tools in orderly rows
5. the metallic twang of a guy line as a worker torques it into place

---

## What they teach

- **Plain, not ornate.** "the oppressive heat and sun" is a complete, good
  color line. Nothing here is over-written. *Detail is not quality* — the
  failed runs were more elaborate than these and worse for it.

- **The place and the work carry the scene.** Four of five are environment
  and technology (wind, heat, the tower, the guy line); only #4 is about
  the crew. Color is not a catalogue of per-NPC tells.

- **The PC's frame of reference is a primary lens.** #2 is color *because
  of who is looking* — a renaissance-era person sees a steel signal tower
  as near-magical. The gap between the PC's world and Kesharat industry is
  a deep, mostly-untapped source. (See `NOTES.md` §4.)

- **Mostly innocent — *because this is the opening.*** Four are pure
  aliveness/wonder; only #4 carries a light dread charge, and it lands
  *because* it's outnumbered. But that ratio is **not an intrinsic value of
  color** — it's what *this beat's job* demands. This is the first thing the
  player stumbles into, so color's work here is to **establish baseline
  normal** and ground the reader in the familiar (a radio tower, workers
  building, a warm day) — standard horror groundwork; you can't feel the
  wrongness until you know what normal was. Deeper in, where a beat already
  asserts something unsettling, color's job flips from *outnumbering* the
  wrong thing to *substantiating* it, and the innocent majority no longer
  applies. **Register is keyed to the beat, not fixed.** (See `NOTES.md` §5.)

- **Sensory range.** Sound (#1, #5), sight (#2, #4), bodily/thermal (#3).
  Not visual-only.

- **A light perception word is fine.** "strange," "oppressive" are the PC's
  honest read and do light work. That differs from a constructed clause
  that *argues* an anomaly (e.g. "no sweat stain *despite the heat*"). The
  first colors; the second explains. (See `NOTES.md` §7.)

- **A bracketed author-note can ride along.** #2's clause about the PC's
  era is a *why* for the curator, not in-game text. Candidates may carry a
  short rationale like this. (See `NOTES.md` §9.)

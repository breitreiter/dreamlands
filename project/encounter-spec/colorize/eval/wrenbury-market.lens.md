# Lens — Wrenbury Market

How the PC sees this material. Steering for color — whose eye, what to notice, in
what key. Not facts (those are in the beats); the *stance*.

## Perceptual
A travelling Guild arbiter with an appraising eye, pricing the market as they walk
it: which relics are genuine and which are dressed-up scrap, whose provenance cards
are honest and whose are invention, what is plainly out of place here (the Revathi
tiles — wrong battlefield, wrong century). The eye that reads a seller's hands and a
buckle's wear, not just the goods. Period frame — a market of waxed cloth, casks,
and handwritten cards, not a modern stall.

## Social / tonal
Mostly ordinary market texture — food, haggling, the bookseller's watchfulness —
with the temperature rising toward the dispute at the end. The PC's signet ring
marks them as Guild: vendors read it and go guarded or hopeful, and the arguing
woman's eye catches on it and *holds*, because it changes what the PC is here. The
seam to notice is the pull from anonymous tourist toward summoned arbiter.

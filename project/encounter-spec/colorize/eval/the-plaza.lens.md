# Lens — The Plaza

How the PC sees this material. Steering for color — whose eye, what to notice, in
what key. Not facts (those are in the beats); the *stance*.

## Perceptual
A renaissance-era traveler who has no word for any of this. The city is a made
thing of a kind they have never seen: stone with no seam or join, light that falls
even and cool from no flame, rooms and stairs sized for something taller than a
person. It reads as the work of something inhuman, near-magical, long gone. Render
it in *their* words, never ours — a dry draught from above, not "ventilation"; rows
of black iron racks, not "industrial shelving"; grooves cut in the floor that run
to nowhere, not "drainage." Torben, the learned companion, measures and theorizes;
the PC mostly feels the wrongness of the proportions. No modern or technical terms.

## Social / tonal
Empty, hushed, oppressive — a place that was built to be lived in and is not. The
beat already asserts the wrong; color substantiates it with the seamless, the
sourceless, the human-incompatible scale, and the statue set at the entrance as if
to watch who comes in. Quiet awe shading into unease. Almost no warmth — only
Torben's lantern and muttering keep it human.

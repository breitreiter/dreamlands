# Lens — The Hermit of Sallow Fen

How the PC sees this material. Steering for color — whose eye, what to notice, in
what key. Not facts (those are in the beats); the *stance*.

## Perceptual
A lost, exhausted traveler who has navigated a trackless rotting fen by instinct and
is bone-tired and hungry. The eye catalogues misery first — black water, biting
flies, rot in the throat — then the shack and the woman, alert to small wrong
details (bones strung on the eaves, her too-clear eyes, her stillness) sitting right
beside things that are genuinely, dangerously inviting (the good smell of the stew,
the fire, a place to sit). Plain frontier world.

## Social / tonal
Horror with the warmth as a lure. Dread is substantiated, not sparse — the beat
already plants something massive shifting unseen in the shack. The trap is that the
comfort is *real*: the stew truly smells good, the fire is truly warm, and the
stomach truly clenches. Color holds both at once — the genuine pull of hospitality
and the wrong notes braided through it — without arguing which is which. The rising
tide and the hunger are quiet pressure.

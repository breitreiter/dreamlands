## Notes

Register mix: grounding-dominant with a dread seam that rises through the scene. The first two-thirds are mundane texture (market smells, food, relics); dread enters with the nameless canteen and peaks with the argument and the ring recognition. No warmth beat in the opening tableau — warmth is gated behind the player choice to buy food, which falls outside the tableau.

Hardest extraction judgment call: "Maybe it does" (re the handwritten provenance cards) and "the seller doesn't seem troubled by that" (re the Revathi tiles). Both are pure voice/editorializing — the author reasoning about meaning rather than showing the thing. The observable underneath each is the card-labeling behavior and the tiles' presence; the editorial clauses were dropped. Similarly "lunch is eternal" is a voice construction built on "smells like every market" — kept only the smell itself.

Lines sitting on the show/argue boundary: the bookseller line is borderline. "face has set that way permanently" is light inference from behavior, not a direct observable. It was kept because the author stated it as a physical fact (a set face) rather than an argued cause — but a stricter read would reduce it to the watching behavior alone.

Didn't fit: the PC's frame-of-reference (renaissance-era world) doesn't appear to bear on this scene the way it does in the Signal Array — there's no Kesharat technology here to estrange. The Revathi tiles are the one out-of-place object, but the strangeness is historical/geographic, not technological, so no PC-frame lens note was needed.
